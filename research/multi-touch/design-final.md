Summary: Chords and hold-and-tap can be relied on wherever #1918's rows deliver concurrent pointers, because under Pointer Events a chord is several pointerIds down at once and needs no recogniser; the one gesture that needs recognising is the hold. The recommended v1 set follows the Digitakt's shape: a press on an empty cell sets it the moment the finger lands, so the cell lights from pointerdown; a quick press on a set cell clears it on release; a 400 ms hold on either opens a velocity strip for the held cell without clearing it (the Elektron, Push and Circuit idiom of hold-the-target-then-choose); a second finger on a strip level sets the velocity and lifting closes the strip, while a single finger that lifts leaves the strip latched for three seconds so the same edit is done sequentially; cells pressed while the strip is open join the selection; a secondary mouse button opens the strip at once on a hover-capable host; pads fire on contact and release, chords being concurrent contacts, with fixed velocity by default. Capability is observed from the pointers actually delivered rather than asked of navigator.maxTouchPoints, which Firefox has reported wrongly in both directions, and the observation changes nothing but a status glyph because the latch already covers the single-contact host. The control contract needs no gesture vocabulary: hold-and-choose is a set of {on, vel} on a cell and a chord is several fires, so #1916's gesture field should be dropped or reduced to a hint naming the field the strip edits, and its drum example must list vel in v1 as #1914 decided. The recommendation departs from the decided wording "hold-one-cell-and-tap-another for accents": the second tap lands on a strip level, not on another cell, and the literal form is on the short list for Simon to pick instead. A tested state machine and a probe page exist under the scratchpad; the hold threshold, pointercancel behaviour and palm rejection on the real panel are hardware-gated.

**Question.** Can chords on pads and hold-one-cell-and-tap-another for accents be relied on, and how does the surface degrade when the host delivers only one contact or a mouse?

## Where this departs from the decision in force

#1912's decision table settles multi-touch as "Supported whenever the host delivers it (chords on pads, hold-one-cell-and-tap-another for accents)". Chords are delivered exactly as decided. For accents, the recommendation below keeps the hold on the target cell but puts the second tap on a velocity strip rather than on another cell, because that is what every grid sequencer read for this design does, it expresses more than one level, and it completes with one finger without a further mode. The literal reading, holding one cell while tapping other cells to accent them, is candidate B and is on the short list with its trade-offs, so Simon's choice from the short list is what settles the wording; the question is returned to the research run for its needs_input task rather than decided here. Nothing on the wire or in the other points depends on which is chosen.

## What the browser delivers, and what that means for gesture design

The browser-delivery finding (#1918) settles where concurrent contacts arrive: Chromium, Edge and Firefox on Windows, Chromium and Firefox on Wayland (GNOME, and labwc once its touch mouse emulation is off), Chromium on X11, and Firefox on X11 only with `MOZ_USE_XINPUT2=1`. This design adds the facts about the Pointer Events model itself that decide how gestures are recognised:

- Each contact is a pointer with its own `pointerId`: "all active pointers in the top-level browsing context must be unique", but "The user agent MAY recycle previously retired values for `pointerId`", so an id identifies a contact only while it is down (https://w3c.github.io/pointerevents/). Chromium reserves `pointerId` 1 for the mouse and gives every finger a fresh id on each `pointerdown`; Firefox uses 0 for the mouse, which is spec-compliant (Mustaq Ahmed on w3c/pointerevents issue 343, https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-pointer-events/2020OctDec/0058.html). A recogniser must therefore key on the id for the life of a contact and on nothing else; the prototype below is tested with both mouse ids.
- `isPrimary`: "The first pointer to become active for a particular pointer type (e.g. the first finger to touch the screen in a multi-touch interaction) becomes the primary pointer for that pointer type", and only primary pointers produce compatibility mouse events (spec, above). `pointerType` is `mouse`, `pen` or `touch` (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Pointer_events). The panel's passive stylus reports as touch and is treated as touch.
- `button` on `pointerdown` is 0 for the primary button or a touch contact and 2 for the secondary mouse button (spec, above, button state table), so a right-click is distinguishable from a press without a second event; `click`, `auxclick` and `contextmenu` are themselves PointerEvents that "the user agents MUST always support", so suppressing the context menu is a separate `contextmenu` listener, which #1918 already requires.
- Implicit capture: touch input "SHOULD behave exactly as if `setPointerCapture` was called on the target element just before the invocation of any `pointerdown` listeners" (spec), and MDN: "an implicit pointer capture will be called on the element when a `pointerdown` event triggers. The capture can be released manually by calling `element.releasePointerCapture` on the target element" (Pointer_events, above; `releasePointerCapture` is Baseline widely available, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Element/releasePointerCapture). Consequence: a finger that lands on cell A and slides to cell B keeps delivering events targeted at A. Hold-and-tap is unaffected (each finger's own `pointerdown` target is the cell it landed on), but painting along a row needs the cell resolved from coordinates (`document.elementFromPoint`, or grid arithmetic on a canvas), which is what the probe page does.
- `pointercancel` fires "when it detects a scenario to suppress a pointer event stream" (spec); under `touch-action: none` the browser owns no pan or zoom gesture, so a cancel should not arrive. Whether a long press with `contextmenu` prevented still cancels the stream is a host behaviour: Firefox on Android used to send a cancel and stopped for web compatibility (https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1481923), and the working group is still shaping long-press context-menu behaviour in 2026 (a January 2026 proposal for a `contextmenuclose` event, https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-pointer-events/2026JanMar/0051.html, which concerns menu closure and shows the area is live rather than settling the cancel question). The recogniser treats a cancel as a discarded contact, never as a release, and the probe counts them; confirming zero on the panel is hardware-gated.
- Capability signals are advisory. `navigator.maxTouchPoints` is 0 on a host without a touchscreen (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/maxTouchPoints), but Firefox returned 0 in content processes on real touchscreens until Firefox 70 (https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1539497, RESOLVED FIXED) and returns 256 on some non-touch Windows desktops today (https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1979581, open, about 0.12 percent of users in Mozilla's data). The media features `(pointer: coarse)` ("The primary input mechanism includes a pointing device of limited accuracy, such as a finger on a touchscreen", https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/pointer) and `(hover: none)` (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/hover) describe the primary input, so a Pi with a mouse plugged in beside the panel reports `fine`. None of these says whether two contacts will arrive; only two contacts arriving says that.
- The degraded hosts look like a mouse. With labwc's mouse emulation on, "all touch up/down/motion events are translated to mouse button and motion events" (#1918), so the page sees one pointer of type `mouse` at a time. That is the single-contact case the brief requires the surface to handle, and it is indistinguishable from a real mouse, which is why the fallback below is not a mode but a property of every gesture.

What generalises: the quick press toggles and the long press edits (the Digitakt sentence is the exact rule); the step is placed on the press, and only its removal waits for a quick release; the held step is the target and the value comes from a second control that appears or is already there (encoders on Push, a knob or parameter on the Digitakt, a pad row on the Circuit); holding several targets edits them together; an accent shortcut, where one exists, is a global fixed-velocity switch rather than a gesture on another step; sixteen velocity levels is the common resolution and 127 is the accent value. Hold-one-step-and-press-another exists in this family for one thing, assigning a note to a step (Circuit, above); none of the manuals read sets note length that way (Push uses encoders while the step is held, the Digitakt its LEN parameter, the Circuit a selected step and a length pad), so length is another value chosen for a held step, not a second-cell gesture. An earlier draft of this design claimed a Push and Digitakt precedent for hold-a-step-and-press-another for length; the manuals do not contain it and the claim is withdrawn.

## What the apps need on the other side

The grid design (#1914) puts velocity in the v1 cell ("v1 fields: presence (on) and `v` velocity") precisely so that the accent gesture has something to write; the contract (#1916) names the field `vel` and makes a cell edit a `set` on `/<app>/<pattern>/grid/<row>/<col>` carrying a partial cell that is merged, so an accent is `{"t":"set","path":".../kick/5","v":{"on":true,"vel":127}}` and no new verb is needed. #1916's drum-grid example declares `"vel": {"type": "midi7", "default": 100}` but lists `"v1": ["on"]`; it must be amended to `"v1": ["on", "vel"]` with a range of 1 to 127 (a velocity of 0 is a note-off in MIDI and in Subsample, below) to match #1914. Subsequence's default velocity is 100 (`subsequence/constants/velocity.py:8`, `DEFAULT_VELOCITY = 100`, the `velocity` default of `hit_steps` at `subsequence/pattern_builder.py:681`), so a strip whose middle level is 100 leaves an unedited cell where it was. A pad chord on Subsample is several independent note-ons: the player picks a velocity layer per note-on (`subsample/player.py:101-106`, "the player scans the list at note-on to find the layer covering `msg.velocity`") and routes a note-off, or a note-on with velocity 0, to `_release_held` (`subsample/player.py:4968-4970`), so each contact maps to one `fire` with `on` and `vel` and its release to `on: false`, the pad example in #1916. The release matters only for held or looping assignments: `_release_held` leaves one-shot voices untouched ("one_shot voices are left untouched", the loop condition `and not voice.one_shot`, `subsample/player.py:5428-5452`), so on a one-shot kit the `on: false` is a no-op and a pad page should not promise that lifting the finger shapes every sound. Auditioning a freshly set cell in the current cycle is possible through `composition.trigger()` ("Trigger a one-shot pattern immediately or on a quantized boundary", `subsequence/composition.py:5224-5300`), which is thread-safe because it ends in `_schedule_one_shot` ("Schedule a one-shot pattern at an absolute pulse, thread-safely", `run_coroutine_threadsafe` when called off the loop, `subsequence/composition.py:5383-5399`); #1914 lists this as already possible and optional, and whether a tap should sound its voice at once is a question for Simon.

## Tap semantics: act on press or on release

A press is ambiguous between a tap and a hold until the finger lifts or the hold threshold passes, so a recogniser that toggles on release cannot light the cell until the finger lifts: the 133 ms mean dwell above is added before the 69 ms budget even starts, and #1912's first use-case requires that "the cell changes state at once to show it is set". The hardware resolves this by acting on the press for the common action and reserving the release for removal, which is the Digitakt rule quoted above and Push's "Pressing a pad in a column will add a note".

| | Toggle on release | Act on press (recommended) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setting an empty cell | on `pointerup`, after the dwell (about 133 ms mean) plus the render budget | on `pointerdown`; the 69 ms budget applies from the finger landing |
| Clearing a set cell | on `pointerup` | on `pointerup` if the press was quick; the hardware does the same, and the cell can show a pressed state from `pointerdown` so the contact is acknowledged at once even though the state change waits |
| Hold on an empty cell | opens the strip and toggles nothing; setting and editing are two gestures | sets the cell and then opens the strip for it: set and edit in one gesture, which is how a Digitakt parameter lock on a new trig works (hold the trig, turn the knob) |
| Hold on a set cell | opens the strip, no toggle | opens the strip, no clear |
| Paint from an empty cell | first cell toggles when the drag is recognised (past the slop) | first cell already set on press; cells entered are set |
| Paint from a set cell | first cell toggles when the drag is recognised | first cell clears when the drag is recognised; cells entered are cleared |
| Mistaken hold on an empty cell | strip opens, nothing set; tap outside | cell is set and the strip opens; tap outside leaves the cell set, and a quick press clears it |
| `pointercancel` after press | nothing was sent | the set already sent stands and is reconciled by the `ack` and `changed` (#1920); a pending clear is dropped |
| Wire | one `set` per tap | one `set` per tap, no more messages |

Act on press is recommended. Its one cost is that a hold that was meant as a tap leaves the cell set, which is the hardware's behaviour too and is undone by a quick press. The prototype implements it and the gesture table below is written in these terms.

B is the literal reading of the decision's "hold-one-cell-and-tap-another" and is excluded from the recommendation for three reasons: no hardware uses the held step as an accent modifier, so a Push or Elektron user would have to learn it; it expresses one level, where the drum-machine idiom and `p.sequence(velocities=)` both carry sixteen; and its single-contact form is a latched switch (D), so it does not escape a mode either, while its two-contact form puts the second finger on the grid under the holding hand. The mode argument does not separate A from B: A's latch is a mode too, a timed and visible one. What separates them is precedent, level count and where the second finger lands. D is kept as a complement to A, not an alternative: a latched accent switch is cheap, single-contact and familiar, and it is listed under the v1 set as optional. C is excluded because it fights the toggle. E is the only other mode-free candidate and it is genuinely attractive for a drum grid, so it is on the short list; against it, the zones are invisible unless drawn, a set cell cannot be re-velocitied without clearing it first, and the vertical axis is the one a melodic row will want for `note`. F is reserved for later, not weighed further here: it separates the value from the cell and costs a lane of height, which is right for melodic rows (where #1916 already has a note lane) and wrong for the first use-case's eight drum rows.

Reserved, not in v1: `len` and `prob` as further rows of the same strip (length is a value chosen for a held step on every device read, above); hold a melodic cell and press a note lane sets `note` (the Circuit's "holding down the step pad and pressing the desired note"; the Digitakt takes the note from an external keyboard while the trig is held); a velocity lane for the selected row (F) for melodic pages; a finger sliding vertically on a held pad as aftertouch; two-finger swipe for page change (pages change by buttons, so a stray swipe cannot change page); pressure, because the panel is projected-capacitive and reports none (the spec's `pressure` is 0.5 for hardware without it). Excluded outright: pinch, long-press menus, double-tap zoom, text selection, all suppressed page-side per #1918.

## Detection strategy: observe, do not ask

The recogniser keeps a map of live pointers by id and records the peak number of non-mouse contacts down at once and the set of `pointerType`s seen. Its state is `unknown` until any contact, `mouse` if only mouse events have arrived (a real mouse, or labwc emulation), `single` after one non-mouse contact at a time, `confirmed` once two have been down together. `navigator.maxTouchPoints` and the media queries are logged beside it as advisory, for the reasons above. Nothing in the gesture set branches on this state: the latch makes every two-contact gesture completable with one, so the same page on a mouse host is slower by one lift and one move, not different, and the secondary button shortens even that. The state is used for two things only: a status glyph so the person at the panel can see that chords are available (or not, on a Pi whose emulation is still on, which is then a host fix per #1921 rather than a page fix), and a note on the pad page when the state is `mouse` or `single` after some use.

A page that listens to pointer events only sees each contact once whether or not it cancels `pointerdown`; the spec's "Authors can prevent the production of certain compatibility mouse events by canceling the `pointerdown` event" matters if a `mousedown` or `mouseup` listener is ever added, and it does not suppress `click` or `contextmenu`, which are PointerEvents in their own right. The probe page calls `preventDefault()` in `pointerdown` for the former reason and suppresses `contextmenu` separately, as #1918 requires. An earlier draft said the call was needed "or every tap arrives twice"; that is not so for a pointer-only page.

The prototype is `/tmp/claude-1000/-home-si/243c6ca9-504c-48de-a06d-eefc5c35da94/scratchpad/multi-touch/gesture.js` (a pure state machine with injected time, running in node and in the browser; the pre-revision toggle-on-release version is kept beside it as `gesture.v1.js`), `gesture_test.js` (eighteen tests, all passing under node 18.19.1 on 2026-09-03, output in `test_output.txt`: set on press and clear on release, on touch and on both mouse ids; hold on a set cell without clearing, hold on an empty cell setting and then editing; the secondary button; the latch and its expiry; two-finger hold-and-choose; multi-hold; single-contact multi-select; tap outside; painting set and painting clear with slop and row lock; a slow press that moves being a paint not a hold; `pointercancel` leaving a sent set standing and dropping a pending clear; pad chords; vertical velocity; and the capability states), and `gesture-probe.html`, which runs the same code against real pointer events with the full suppression set, hit-tests by coordinates, passes `button` through, and records `pointercancel` counts, `contextmenu` suppression, pinch leakage, selection leakage, `maxTouchPoints`, the media queries and the observed peak. They are research prototypes, not product code and not house style.

## How the contract expresses a gesture that needs two contacts

It does not, and should not. A gesture is a page-side way of producing an effect the contract already names: hold-and-choose is a `set` of `{on, vel}` on a cell, multi-hold is several such sets, a chord is several `fire`s in flight at once with independent releases, and painting is several sets. Nothing on the wire says how many fingers made them, and the service, the adapter and Subsequence are the same whether the host has ten contacts or a mouse. What the contract must provide: the cell field `vel` writable in v1 with a range and default (decided by #1914; #1916's example needs the `v1` list and range amended as noted above); a partial cell merged by `set`; `fire` carrying `on` and `vel` per pad with no coupling between pads; `applies` so the strip can show a pending level until `changed` confirms it (all present in #1916). Two additions are proposed for #1916, both hints: on a `grid` leaf, an optional `levels` array under a cell field (for example `"vel": {"type": "midi7", "range": {"min": 1, "max": 127}, "default": 100, "levels": [40, 70, 100, 127]}`) so the strip's rows come from the app's declaration when the app has an opinion (a drum machine with three accent levels, a synth with 16), falling back to a page-side default; and on a `trigger` leaf with `hint: pad`, an optional `velocity: "fixed" | "vertical"` widget hint with a `fixed_vel` value. The `gesture: {modifier: "hold"}` field that #1916 left for this point to fill is proposed for removal: a hold is what the surface does to any cell with a writable field beyond `on`, so the field would repeat information the cell schema already carries, and a per-leaf gesture vocabulary would have to grow with every widget. If a hint is wanted at all, `edit: ["vel"]` naming the fields the strip shows, in order, is the honest minimum.

## Trade-off table across the gesture families

| | Chords on pads | Hold-and-choose on cells | Multi-hold selection | Paint along a row |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Needs concurrent contacts | yes for a chord; each note alone needs one | no (latch, or the secondary button) | no (latched selection) | no |
| Recogniser | none | hold timer plus strip state | same state | slop plus coordinate hit-test |
| Failure on a mouse host | chords impossible, single hits fine | one extra lift, or none with the secondary button | one extra lift | fine |
| Failure when a cancel arrives | voice released | contact discarded, strip latched | same | paint stops |
| Prior art | every pad controller | Push, Digitakt, Circuit, Maschine | Push, Digitakt | touch apps, not hardware |
| Wire | independent `fire`s | `set` per cell | `set` per cell | `set` per cell |

## Short list

Letters are the candidate table's.

| | A. Hold the target, choose a level on a strip (recommended) | B. Held cell as accent modifier, with the accent switch D as its one-contact form | E. Velocity from where the tap lands in the cell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Matches the decided wording | in spirit: hold the cell, tap something else | literally | no: one finger, no hold |
| Hardware idiom | Push, Digitakt, Circuit, Maschine | none for accent | none at cell level |
| Levels | any, declared by the app or four by default; two (default and 127) is the smallest honest strip | one | two or three |
| Two-handed accent placement across a row | one hold plus one tap per cell, or multi-hold then one tap | fastest: hold one cell, tap the others | one tap per cell, no hold |
| One-contact host | latch (3 s, visible) or the secondary button | the accent switch, lit | as is |
| Re-velocity a set cell | hold and choose | no | no, without clearing first |
| Contract | `set` `{on, vel}`; `levels` hint optional | `set` `{on, vel}` | `set` `{on, vel}` |
| Risk | the hold threshold (too short and slow presses open the strip; too long and the edit feels sticky) | learning cost; one level; second finger under the hand | zone boundaries invisible; the vertical axis spent |

## Recommendation

A, with D as an optional switch on the page and the tap acting on press. It is the option that is at once the hardware idiom (so a Push or Elektron user needs no teaching), complete on a single-contact host with a visible timed mode rather than a hidden one (so the labwc-emulation Pi, a laptop trackpad and a mouse all work, only slower, and a right button skips the wait), and silent on the wire (so #1914, #1916 and #1920 need nothing but the two hints above and the `v1` amendment, and even the hints are optional). It departs from the decided wording as stated at the top, and B is there for Simon to pick if two-handed accent spraying matters more than levels and precedent; E is there if a hold is unwanted altogether on a drum grid. Its risk is the hold threshold: 400 ms is the platform norm and the probe page is where it is tuned. Fixed pad velocity by default, vertical velocity as a page option, because a finger on glass carries no velocity and a guessed one is worse than a chosen one.

## Hardware-gated

- On the chosen host, with `gesture-probe.html`: peak concurrent contacts reaches 10 with emulation off (Pi) or out of the box (Windows, Ubuntu); the observed state reaches `confirmed`.
- `pointercancel` count stays at zero through long presses with `contextmenu` prevented, on Chromium and Edge on Windows, on Firefox on Windows, and on Chromium under labwc; if a host cancels on long press, the hold gesture on that host must be recognised before the cancel arrives or the threshold shortened.
- Under labwc with mouse emulation still on, whether a long press arrives as a secondary-button mouse `pointerdown` (the compositor's long-press-for-right-click), which would open the strip on the emulated host as a side effect; for the record.
- Whether Windows draws its touch indicator ring during a hold, and whether the kiosk account should have it off: Settings > Accessibility > Mouse pointer & touch > Touch indicator ("Windows can also show visual feedback when you touch the screen", https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/windows/make-windows-easier-to-see).
- Palm rejection (the datasheet says yes, #1917) with the heel of the hand resting while a second finger works the strip; the panel's report rate, which bounds how quickly a second contact is seen.
- The hold threshold and the latch time as felt on the panel; the strip's placement relative to the holding hand so the second finger does not cross it; whether a pressed state on a set cell reads as acknowledgement while the clear waits for the release.
- Tap-to-light time on the Pi 5 from `pointerdown` against the 69 ms direct-tapping JND and drag-to-paint against 11 ms, jointly with `ui-rendering` and `latency`.
- What `navigator.maxTouchPoints` reports under labwc with emulation on and off, for the record only.
