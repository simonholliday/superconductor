Summary: The recommended page model's undesigned parts are filled in and one of them is corrected. Arrangement: the short list is a vendored Gridstack 13.2.0, a bespoke snap-drag that also takes a tap-to-place, tap-to-place alone, or no on-panel arrangement in v1, with a variant that keeps Gridstack's layout model and turns its drag layer off; the bespoke engine is recommended, and Gridstack is declined not on the 300 ms touch delay a first pass attributed to it — that constant is on master's unreleased 13.2.0-dev tree and in no release — but on `if (e.touches.length > 1) return;` at the released tag, a `releasePointerCapture` on every non-mouse pointer, and a `_fixCollisions` that resolves overlap by moving widgets the hand did not touch and has no refusal branch, which is in the engine and survives switching the drag layer off. The replacement is 105 non-comment lines passing seventeen assertions on a 32-by-18 lattice of 60 px cells that tiles the 1920x1080 panel exactly; the lattice places widgets and does not divide their insides, which is what keeps #2018's 32-step grid view and a 32-column lattice from colliding, and a page declares its own lattice so #1998 moving the 60 px costs a re-author rather than a silent reinterpretation. A smaller viewport scales uniformly to fit and says when its cell falls under the enhanced touch target. Routes: five `/api/pages` routes ahead of the static `Mount` on #1929's table, a single PUT whose `If-None-Match: *` creates and `If-Match` replaces, no precondition answered 428, a stale save answered 412 carrying the current strong ETag (SHA-256 of the bytes), a whole-strip conditional reorder that writes all or nothing because `order` lives inside each document, a per-page `asyncio.Lock` around a temp-file-plus-`os.replace` write, and two new frames on #1920's socket whose `op` field is what makes a create or a delete on one panel reach another's page bar; 42 assertions pass against Starlette 1.6.0. This takes the compare-and-swap discipline #1925 rejected for grid cells, on the document side of the line #1925 itself drew. The browser half is designed with it: what `localStorage` holds, what the panel draws when the service is unreachable, and which precondition a save built on a cached copy carries, in 21 assertions. Naming: generated names from #1916's candidates plus a page-drawn key grid with no focusable input, which also takes hardware keystrokes wherever a keyboard exists — not because an on-screen keyboard is impossible on the Pi, but because reaching one costs either the fullscreen kiosk #1921 chose or a forked squeekboard build, and buys a surface the page cannot control. Edit mode is a latched control outside the grid whose scrim keeps every arrange contact away from the cell recogniser, and tap-to-place is the same engine with no drag at all.
