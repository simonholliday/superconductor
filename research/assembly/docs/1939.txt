#1939  The later pages reach Subsample and Substation through the same adapter and contract with three small additions: pads are fire frames the Subsample adapter turns into MIDI on its own virtual port, waveforms travel inline as a 400-bin envelope value, a records value type carries recording and sample lists, and the band selector is a restart Substation performs itself by stopping its scanner and constructing a fresh one in the same process, so the service owns no process
  design · active · #research · #subsample-substation · superintendent

Summary: Each later page maps onto #1916's leaves and #1928's adapter without a per-app transport: the pad page is trigger leaves whose fire frames the Subsample adapter converts to note-on and note-off on a mido output into the process's own virtual port (measured 0.24 ms hop; a co-located service sending MIDI itself would save about 1 ms against a 3 to 23 ms output buffer, so the direct path is not worth its dependency, and network MIDI is estimated at the same order as the adapter path across a LAN); the waveform page draws Subsample's sidecar envelope carried inline as the leaf's value (3.7 KB) rather than as a file the service must be able to read, and a Substation recording gets the same envelope computed by its adapter from the final WAV; the band page is a select with applies restart, performed by Substation itself: the adapter's request lands on the scanner loop, the running RadioScanner stops through the sentinel it already has, and a loop in run_scanner constructs a fresh RadioScanner for the new band, keeping the link up and the service out of process management (measured 40 ms plus the 2 s warm-up with a file device on the workstation; an in-process retune is deferred, and the service owning a Substation child, which the first draft recommended, is excluded because it inverts #1919's registration posture and #1929's unit ordering). The contract needs three MINOR additions: an envelope value type for waveform leaves, a records value type with a list kind for recordings and sample libraries, and a mode field on triggers; the service needs no new feature.

**Question.** How is each later page (Subsample pads, Subsample waveforms and library, Substation band, channels and recordings) reached, and does the adapter shape of #1928 with the declaration schema of #1916 carry them without special cases?

## Ground

#1919 puts one service on the apps' network, co-located by default and identical on another host, with the apps dialling the service (each app holds one address, the service's; the service's configuration names the apps it expects and nothing about where they run); #1929 runs the service as a systemd unit with no `After=` or `Requires=` on the music apps; #1920 gives the browser one WebSocket with JSON envelopes (about 1 ms round-trip on loopback, about 4 ms to connect, about 1 ms more across a wired LAN); #1916 declares `trigger`, `number`, `select`, `meter`, `waveform` and `text` leaves with `applies` in `immediate`, `next_cycle`, `restart`; #1928 puts one adapter module in each app on a daemon thread that dials the service and, for Subsample, sends pad MIDI into the process's own virtual port, leaving the co-located direct-MIDI path to this point, and for Substation forwards a band-switch verb with `call_soon_threadsafe`. The companion findings establish what each app has and lacks: Subsample triggers only by MIDI, emits only `cc`, and carries a 400-bin envelope block in every sidecar; Substation has no inbound path, band fixed per process, no waveform data, a clean stop its scanner already performs on a queued sentinel, and a restart that costs about 0.75 s of imports plus a device open plus about 13.7 s of RTL-SDR calibration when enabled plus a 2 s warm-up.

## Pad triggering

| | Service opens a mido output to the virtual port when co-located (#1916's `midi` block) | Subsample adapter receives `fire` and sends MIDI into its own process's virtual port (#1928's standard) | Network MIDI to the sampler host (RTP-MIDI) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hops after the browser frame reaches the service | ALSA sequencer only | one WebSocket frame on loopback (about 1 ms, #1920), a queue handoff to the adapter's sender, then ALSA (0.235 ms median, 0.289 ms p99 measured) | RTP over UDP to `rtpmidid` or the OS's own RTP-MIDI, then ALSA |
| Works when the service is on another host (#1919's shape B) | no | yes, unchanged: the WebSocket frame crosses the LAN instead of loopback | yes, that is its purpose |
| Dependencies | `mido` and `python-rtmidi` in the service, a process on the same ALSA sequencer as the sampler | none new (`mido` is Subsample's) | `rtpmidid` (GPLv3 daemon, LGPL 2.1 library, https://github.com/davidmoreno/rtpmidid) on the sampler host and an RTP-MIDI sender in the service, RFC 6295 (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6295.html) |
| Subsample's lock model | untouched | untouched: the rtmidi callback thread stays the only MIDI handler | untouched |
| Who releases a held pad when the panel disconnects | the service must send note-off | the adapter tracks the pads it holds and sends note-off on link loss; the service also sends `fire` with `on: false` when a panel drops | the service |
| Latency, co-located | ALSA hop only, about 0.24 ms | reference: about 1 ms plus 0.24 ms | not applicable |
| Latency, service on another host | not available | one LAN WebSocket frame (about 1 ms software plus about 1 ms wire, #1920) plus the 0.24 ms hop, about 2 ms, not measured across a LAN | not measured; estimated as one UDP hop (the wired-neighbour ICMP round trip in #1920 was 0.48 ms, 1.76 ms through the router), `rtpmidid`'s receive path (it plays on arrival: its README lists "Use rtp midi timestamps" under its roadmap and recommends Ethernet because "with WiFi there is a lot more latency"; McLaren Labs' rtpmidi defaults its latency adjustment to 0 and can only delay a note, never advance it, https://mclarenlabs.com/blog/2024/08/31/rtpmidi-version-2-0-latency-adjustment/), then the same ALSA hop: about 1 to 2 ms one way on a wired LAN, so within about a millisecond of the middle column and with a daemon to run |

The end-to-end pad budget as far as it can be assembled today: browser to service about 1 ms on loopback plus about 1 ms of wired LAN (#1920), service to adapter about 1 ms on loopback, adapter to Subsample's callback 0.24 ms, Subsample's dispatch under 1 ms (`README.md:2100-2110`), then the output buffer of 2.7 to 23 ms depending on `buffer_frames` (`README.md:2113-2127`), the figure printed at startup being ground truth. The direct path saves about 1 ms of a budget whose largest term is the output buffer, and only when the service shares the sampler's host; network MIDI saves nothing measurable over the adapter path across a LAN and adds a daemon. The recommendation is therefore the middle column as the only pad path in v1, with the `midi` block kept in the declaration as documentation of what the adapter sends rather than as a service instruction; the left column is not excluded from the contract, only from the build, and can be revisited when `latency` measures the real host. The Web MIDI API in the browser is excluded because the panel host has no MIDI route to the sampler.

What the manifest producer emits: one `trigger` per note of each assignment, since a `notes:` list is several sounds by similarity rank (`README.md:706-713`), labelled by the assignment name and rank; `midi: {channel, note}` for the adapter; and a `mode` field (`one_shot`, `gated`, `loop`) so the panel knows whether holding a pad has any meaning (a `one_shot` voice ignores note-off, `player.py:5527`, `README.md:495`). A `fire` with `{on: true, vel}` becomes `note_on`, `{on: false}` becomes `note_off`; the adapter keeps the set of `(channel, note)` it has pressed and releases them on link loss. This adds one field to #1916's `trigger` and is a MINOR change.

## Waveform display

| | Envelope inline as the leaf's value | `blob_ref` to the sidecar or WAV, served by the service from an allowlist (Supervisor's `/audio/` pattern, `supervisor/core.py:131-172`) | PNG (Subsample's `.preview.png`) | Browser decodes the WAV |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bytes per sample on the wire | 3.6 KB median, 10 KB max measured over 5387 samples (two 400-entry int8 arrays as base64, four band arrays, times and colour) | a path; then 3.6 to 100 KB for the sidecar or 65 KB to 2 MB for the audio | 1.9 to 11 KB, median 4.5 KB, inline as base64 or as a served file | 65 KB to 2 MB |
| Works when the service cannot read the app's files (another host, no shared filesystem) | yes | no | yes if the adapter carries it inline as base64, exactly as the envelope; no if the service serves the file | no |
| Browser cost measured (headless Chromium 151) | fetch 0.8 ms, decode 0.1 ms, draw 0.05 ms | as inline plus one fetch | an `img`, not measured | decode 0.95 ms for 2 s, 8.7 ms for 64 s, envelope 0.1 to 2 ms |
| Theme and scale | drawn by the panel at any size and colour | as inline | fixed 1024 by 256 in Subsample's colours (`preview.py:63-64`) | drawn by the panel |
| Producer | Subsample writes the block on capture and import (`subsample/cache.py:562-609`); the adapter reads `cache_path(record.filepath)`; Substation's adapter computes the same two arrays from the final WAV in about 1 ms | the app or the service | Subsample only; Substation has no raster and would need one drawn | none |

Recommended: the envelope inline. #1916 declared `waveform` with `value: "blob_ref"` and left the fetch to this point; the finding is that the block is small enough to be the value, which removes the service's need to read either app's files and makes the Subsample and Substation waveforms one widget. The PNG carried inline would work off-host just as well, at 4.5 KB against 3.6 KB, and needs no drawing code; it loses on theme, scale and on having no Substation producer, which is why the envelope is chosen and not because the PNG cannot travel. Subsample's `render_svg` (`preview.py:704`) was measured and is excluded as a wire format: 34 to 67 KB of SVG at 800 by 200 against the 3.6 KB block it is drawn from, at a fixed size and in Subsample's colours, so it is a rendering of the block that the panel can do itself. The contract change is a MINOR addition of a value type `envelope`: `{"bins": 400, "min": "<base64 int8>", "max": "<base64 int8>", "duration_s": 1.8, "bands": [...optional...], "accent": "#3fb950", "ref": "<asset path, optional>"}`, with `format: "envelope_400"` kept on the leaf. `blob_ref` stays for the audio file itself, used by an audition control when the service is configured with the app's directories; when it is not, the audition control is absent and nothing else changes. The Subsample adapter fills the value from `sample_captured`'s `filepath` and, for the library, from `instrument_library.samples()`; the Substation adapter computes it in its `recording_saved` handler on its own thread (the file is final when the event fires, `scanner.py:1458-1523`) and is the recommended producer over a `recording.py` change or Subsample's `/sample/import`, which costs 0.3 to 4.7 s of analysis per file and a running sampler.

Lists. A library of samples and a history of recordings are collections, and #1916 has no collection leaf: a `select` enumerates ids, a `meter` carries a series aligned to rows, an `event` is transient. The second MINOR addition is `kind: "list"` with `value: "records"`, an ordered array of objects each carrying an `id` and declared fields (for recordings: `at`, `band`, `channel`, `freq_mhz`, `duration_s`, `envelope`, `ref`; for samples: `name`, `duration_s`, `pitch`, `envelope`, `ref`), `range: {max_len}` bounding the array, `snapshot` carrying the current array and `changed` carrying either the array or a `{"op": "append", "v": record}` patch. Supervisor kept a `deque(maxlen=20)` of the same records (`supervisor/app/substation.py:76`, `supervisor/app/subsample.py:86`); the Substation adapter fills its snapshot by scanning `audio_output_dir/YYYY-MM-DD/<band>/` (`recording.py:377`) because no API lists past recordings.

## Substation band switching

| | Substation stops its scanner and constructs a fresh `RadioScanner` in the same process (a loop in `run_scanner`) | Process restart by whatever runs Substation today (its shell or its own unit), band from configuration or argv | systemd template unit `substation@<band>.service`, the service calls `systemctl` | In-process retune verb mutating `RadioScanner` in place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost of a switch | device close and reopen, RTL-SDR calibration about 13.7 s when enabled, warm-up 2 s; measured with the package's file device on the workstation at 37 to 41 ms from request to the new band's first slice and about 150 ms to warm-up complete, processing-bound (companion finding) | the same plus about 0.75 s of imports, and the link drops | the same as the restart | warm-up 2 s plus stream restart; saves the device reopen and the calibration |
| What the panel sees | `select` with `applies: restart`: pending until `changed` arrives with the new band; the link stays up, so no `app {present: false}` interval; the channel rows change by `path_removed` and `path_added` or a fresh `manifest` | pending; `app {present: false}` in between (#1920); manifest and snapshot re-sent when the new process registers | the same | pending until `changed`; link stays up |
| Code | about a dozen lines in `substation/cli.py` around the single construction at `cli.py:150-167`, a public stop wrapping `_signal_stream_end` (`scanner.py:941-969`), the adapter re-registering its handlers on each instance; optionally a constructor argument carrying the measured ppm so calibration is not repaid | none in Substation; but no route from a panel `set` to a new argv without a wrapper script or a config edit and a self-exit, which is this column re-inventing the first with a dropped link | a unit file; the call from the service needs authorisation for a system unit or a user-level unit ("Unit names can be parameterized by a single argument called the instance name", https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/systemd.unit.5.html; `Restart=` and `RestartSec=` default 100 ms, https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/systemd.service.5.html) | new code in Substation: stop recordings, cancel and restart the device read, drain the queue, rebuild the band block and FFT parameters, reset the noise-floor state, all between two executor slices; device retune-while-streaming unstated for HackRF and SoapySDR |
| When Substation runs on a Pi and the service elsewhere | identical: the adapter forwards the `set` with `call_soon_threadsafe` and nothing else moves | identical | the unit lives on the Pi; the service needs a way to call it there | identical |
| Fit with #1919 and #1929 | the app dials the service and the service holds no app configuration, as both require | as required | the service must know the unit's host and hold a privilege to control it | as required |
| Failure | a switch that fails in `_setup_sdr` (device absent, bad band) ends `scan()` and the loop can fall back to the previous band or exit to its unit's `Restart=`; the argv band is what a crash restart returns to unless the last band is persisted | systemd's `Restart=on-failure` or a hand restart | systemd's `Restart=on-failure` | a failed retune must fall back to a stop and a fresh instance anyway |
| Fit with #1916 | `applies: restart` exists for exactly this and its panel semantics are unchanged; the leaf may move to `next_cycle` later without any page changing, as #1916 says | `applies: restart` | the same | `next_cycle` |

Recommended for v1: the first column. It is #1928's Substation rule applied to a stop the class already has, it keeps the adapter link up, it works identically on a Pi, and it asks nothing of the service, which stays a peer the apps dial rather than a supervisor of their processes. The retune verb is deferred, not refused: the finding shows it saves the device reopen and the calibration and nothing else, its rebuild list runs across the executor boundary of a 2000-line class, and its device behaviour is unverified for two of three bindings; it is a Substation wave under #611, to be revisited if a switch measured on the real hardware is too slow for the page. The systemd template is kept as the alternative for a deployment where Simon wants systemd to own the band as well as the process, at the price of a privilege story. Excluded, and named because the first draft of this document recommended it: the service owning a Substation child process and restarting it with `--band`. It inverts #1919's registration posture (the apps dial the service; the service's configuration says nothing about where the apps are) and #1929's unit ordering (no `After=` or `Requires=` on the music apps), it would make the service hold Substation's venv path, config path and device arguments, and on a Pi it would need a fourth kind of process, a runner, that no other document has; nothing it offered survives the first column, which gives the panel the same leaf with the link kept up. Two mitigations cost nothing now: `calibration_frequency_hz: null` in a panel-switched deployment removes the 13.7 s from every route but the retune, and a second device is a second process on its own band, which is Substation's own one-process-per-device shape (`README.md:839-852`), so a panel that switches often between two bands can be given both at once.

| Kit (Subsample programs) | `select`, enum ids are program numbers | `set` | send `program_change` | `active_bank` on request; a `program` event once emitted |

| Channels (Substation) | `meter`, `series`, `rate_hz: 4` | none | `channel_snr` handler on the executor thread swaps a reference; the link thread samples it | `telemetry` |
| Channel activity with tone | `event`, plus read-only `toggle` per channel | none | `channel_state` handler landing on the loop | `event` and `changed` |
| Recordings (Substation) | `list` of `records` with `envelope`; `event` for started and discarded | none | `recording_saved` handler on the loop hands the path to the link thread, which reads the WAV and computes the 400-bin envelope in about 1 ms | `changed` append |

Every row uses #1928's per-app rule (locked reads and MIDI in for Subsample, sync handlers and reference swaps for Substation, `call_soon_threadsafe` for the band request) and #1916's verbs. The special cases, named so they are not hidden: `fire` release on disconnect is adapter state, already #1928's rule; the `cc` echo rule is adapter state too; and the three MINOR contract additions above (`envelope` value, `list` kind with `records` value, `mode` on `trigger`). Nothing needs a per-app transport, a second codec, or a service that manages a process.

## Short list

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Adapter-only pads, inline envelopes, same-process band switch** (recommended): the Subsample adapter sends pad MIDI into its own virtual port; waveform and list values travel inline over the adapter link; Substation's `run_scanner` loops over fresh `RadioScanner`s on the adapter's request | works wherever the service runs; no file access, MIDI stack or process manager in the service; one waveform widget for both apps; the band link never drops; three MINOR contract additions and a dozen lines in Substation's CLI | a switch still costs about 2 s plus a device reopen, or about 16 s with RTL-SDR calibration enabled; handlers must be re-registered per scanner instance; the argv band and the running band can differ |
| **Co-located optimisations added**: the same, plus the service sending pad MIDI directly and serving files from allowlisted directories when it shares the host | about 1 ms off a pad hit; audition from the panel; PNG thumbnails | `mido` and `python-rtmidi` in the service; two pad paths to test; directory configuration; nothing works off-host |
| **Substation retune verb now**: the same as the first, with a `retune(band)` coroutine in `RadioScanner` instead of a fresh instance | unbroken stream as well as link; saves the device reopen and the calibration | new code across the scanner's executor boundary; device behaviour while streaming unverified for HackRF and SoapySDR; a Substation wave before the panel can switch bands |

Excluded: the panel driving Subsample over OSC or a new Subsample listener, by #1928 (OSC stays the user door) and by Subsample's own design (OSC cannot trigger playback, `subsample/osc.py:141-157`); the service owning a Substation child process, for the posture reasons in the band section.

## Recommendation

The first option. Reasons: the pad hop measured inside the process is 0.24 ms, so the direct-MIDI shortcut buys about a millisecond against an output buffer of 3 to 23 ms and would tie pads to co-location, and network MIDI buys nothing measurable across a LAN; the envelope block is 3.6 KB and draws in a tenth of a millisecond, so carrying it inline removes the whole question of the service reading the apps' files; and Substation's scanner already stops cleanly on a sentinel, so a loop in its CLI switches bands with the link up for the cost the hardware imposes (device reopen, calibration if enabled, warm-up), leaving the service a peer the apps dial rather than a process manager, which is what #1919 and #1929 built on.

## Hardware-gated

- End-to-end pad latency on the real sampler host for the adapter path (and the direct path if it is ever built), once `latency` has its harness; RTP-MIDI across a real LAN if shape B is ever deployed.
- Band-switch wall-clock on the headless server and on a Pi with each device, with and without calibration, including the device close-and-reopen the same-process route pays.
- Retune-while-streaming behaviour of the RTL-SDR, HackRF and AirSpy bindings, before any retune verb is written.

## Not covered

- The `latency` point had not filed when this was written; the pad budget above is assembled from #1920 and this point's own measurements.
- RTP-MIDI was not measured; its row is an estimate from #1920's LAN figures and the daemons' own statements.
- Whether an unprivileged service may restart a system unit through polkit was not verified; the recommendation no longer needs it.
- The `list` and `envelope` additions are proposed shapes, not test vectors; the conformance table of #1928 should gain rows for them.
- The same-process switch was executed with a file device and no open recording; the close-recordings step of the stop is read, not run (companion finding).
