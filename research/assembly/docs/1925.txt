#1925  Grid sync is absolute sets applied in loop order under one version, confirmed by ack at application and by a per-cycle event carrying the built version, a state word and the realised cells; the algorithm's hand is ephemeral by default and persistent when an edit is meant to last; a cell's face is the server's value and a ring is the human's unconfirmed request
  design · active · #research · #state-sync · superintendent

Summary: Bidirectional sync between the panel and Subsequence is last-writer-wins in the order the composition loop applies writes, with #1920's per-app `ver` as that order made visible and no client-side base version; compare-and-set and multi-value registers were weighed and rejected because absolute sets already make a stale write harmless. A tap is confirmed twice: `ack` plus `changed` the moment the helper applies the intent on the loop, and a per-cycle `cycle` event carrying the version the rebuild consumed (`built_ver`), a `state` word and the realised cells, which turns every accepted write at or below `built_ver` into settled or hollow. `built_ver` is stamped in `grid.apply()` when the builder runs and falls back to the version recorded at `reschedule_pulse` when it does not, because a muted or energy-gated pattern skips the builder while `pattern_reschedule` still fires (measured, H7). Every `ack`, `changed` and `cycle` frame enters one ordered log on the loop at the moment of application and a link thread drains it, because a panel write can land between a rebuild and its listener and would otherwise leave the process before the algorithm's earlier write (measured, H8). The algorithm's hand has three shapes: writing intent unconstrained, writing intent with a one-cycle yield guard, or contributing ephemerally beside `grid.apply()` so its notes reach the panel through the realised map without touching intent (measured, H9); the ephemeral shape is recommended as the default idiom and the persistent shapes when an edit is meant to last. A cell's face is always the server's value and a ring is the human's unconfirmed request, chosen over an optimistic face for the invariant that every panel agrees on every face. Resync is #1920's snapshot extended with `built_ver`, `state` and the realised map; pending sets younger than the 5 s timeout are re-sent after it and the (client, seq) dedup repeats their original `ack`.

**Question.** What are the exact semantics of bidirectional sync between the panel and Subsequence when a human on glass and an algorithm in the composition write the same grid, two panels may be open, and a connection may drop mid-tap?

## What the engine fixes before any design

Every decision below follows from six facts about the code.

- **There is one writer thread.** Every write that reaches the grid is applied on the composition's asyncio loop: panel writes because the `StepGrid` helper of #1914 marshals them there with `call_soon_threadsafe` (the `unregister()` precedent, `composition.py:4020-4030`), and algorithm writes because they happen inside the builder, which `_rebuild()` runs on the loop (`composition.py:5989-5998`, `6043-6092`). Writes therefore have a total order without any clock, and "last writer" means "applied later on the loop".
- **The rebuild is the commit point, and the algorithm runs inside it.** `_maybe_reschedule_patterns` fires repeating callbacks first (`sequencer.py:1690-1716`), awaits `reschedule_pulse` (`sequencer.py:1804`; ordering pinned by `tests/test_sequencer_events.py:10-51`), then calls `on_reschedule()` per pattern and spawns `pattern_reschedule` after each (`sequencer.py:1810-1829`). A builder that writes to the grid writes after every panel write applied since the previous rebuild and before the cells are realised. Within one cycle the algorithm is the last writer by construction; the only way a human's edit survives an algorithm that overwrites the same cell is for the builder to leave it alone.
- **A muted or gated pattern skips the builder but not the confirmation hook.** `_rebuild()` empties `steps` and returns at `if self._muted: return` (`composition.py:6010-6011`) and again at the energy gate (`composition.py:6020-6032`) before the `PatternBuilder` is constructed at `6043`, so nothing the builder would do happens, including any call into the helper; `_spawn(pattern_reschedule)` at `sequencer.py:1829` sits outside the `try` and fires regardless. A pattern removed by `unregister()` is skipped entirely (`sequencer.py:1786-1787`) and gets no confirmation; #1916's `path_removed` covers it.
- **Intent and realised are two things.** The grid in `composition.data` is intent; `pattern.steps` after a rebuild is what will sound (`pattern.py:147`, `162-190`). Between them act probability, a per-call roll inside `hit_steps` and `sequence` (`pattern_builder.py:723`, `1248`), mute and the energy gate (the two returns above), a builder that raised (`_rebuild` discards a half-built pattern, `composition.py:6094-6101`), and any note the builder places beside the grid. A snapshot taken between a rebuild and its cycle start can hold an intent the coming cycle will not play (H5).
- **Confirmation has a natural carrier and no per-step event.** `pattern_reschedule(pattern, cycle_start_pulse)` is spawned as a task straight after the rebuild (`sequencer.py:932-944`, `1829`) and runs one lookahead before the cycle starts (#1915); there is nothing finer (`sequencer.py:1416`, `1427` for `bar` and `beat`). Because it is a spawned task, a callback already in the loop's ready queue when the pulse handler runs, which is what a `call_soon_threadsafe` panel write arriving during the pulse is, executes after the rebuild and before the listener (H8).
- **The engine keeps no edit count.** `live_info()` reports values, not versions (`composition.py:3868-3915`); the version is the helper's (#1914) carried by the service (#1920), so this design decides what the numbers mean, not whether they exist.

## Three moments, and which frame carries each

A tap passes through three moments and the protocol names all three, because the panel needs a different look for each.

| Moment | Meaning | Carrier | When, on a wired LAN |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Applied | the helper wrote the intent on the loop; `ver` advanced | `ack` to the sender naming `seq` and the new `ver`; `changed` with `by` and `client` to every panel including the sender | a few milliseconds after the tap: the browser hop measured 0.8 to 1 ms loopback round-trip plus about 1 ms wire (#1920); the service-to-app hop and the marshal onto the loop were not timed (#1919 measured that hop's cost to the clock, nil on a thread, not its latency) and are loopback again, so of the same order |
| Built | a rebuild consumed the intent, or skipped it; the cells that will sound are known | an `event` named `cycle` on the grid path carrying `built_ver`, `state`, the cycle index, the cycle start pulse and the realised cells | within a pulse of the rebuild, so between a pulse and one cycle after the tap with a one-pulse lookahead (H4); one lookahead before the cycle starts (#1915) |
| Sounding | the step is being played | `playhead`'s business; an overlay on any cell state | one lookahead plus the cell's offset after the build |

**How `built_ver` is stamped.** Inside `grid.apply(p)` the helper records `built_ver = ver`, `built_cycle` and `applied = True`; the stamp is synchronous on the loop and comes after the algorithm's writes made before `apply()` in that builder, so every version at or below it is in the realised map and every version above it is not. The `reschedule_pulse` listener, awaited before any rebuild on that pulse (`sequencer.py:1804`), records `pre_ver = ver` for every grid whose pattern is in `patterns` and clears `applied`. The `pattern_reschedule` listener then emits `cycle` with `built_ver` from the `apply()` stamp when `applied` is set, and otherwise with `pre_ver`, because a muted or energy-gated pattern never reaches `apply()` (H7) and nor does a builder that raised before calling it. `pre_ver` is at or below the true pre-rebuild version (a marshalled write can land between the two listeners only if some `reschedule_pulse` listener is a coroutine, since `emit_async` gathers those, `event_emitter.py:75-109`), so the fallback can under-report by a write, which leaves a ring armed one cycle longer, and can never over-report, which would settle a ring for a build that did not take it. A builder that writes intent after calling `apply()` writes for the next cycle and the panel draws that cell hollow until then. `state` is `built` when `applied` is set, `muted` when `pattern._muted` is true (the flag `live_info()` reports, `composition.py:3868-3915`), `gated` when `_energy_gated` is true (`composition.py:6023-6029`), and `silent` otherwise, which covers a builder that raised before `apply()` and a builder that never calls it; a builder that raised after `apply()` is not distinguishable from one that placed nothing (Not covered).

**How the realised map is derived.** In the `pattern_reschedule` listener, on the loop: iterate `pattern.steps`, a dict of pulse offset to `Step` (`pattern.py:147`); for each `Note`, the row is `note.origin` when the pitch was named (`pattern.py:40`, set by `add_note`, `pattern.py:162-190`) and otherwise the first name in the pattern's `drum_note_map` that maps to the pitch; the column is the offset divided by `length × 24 / cols` when that divides exactly, with `cols` the grid's own step count, which is also what the builder passes as `grid=`. A note whose offset is not on a column, from a triplet `grid=` or a nudge, is counted in `off_grid` and not drawn, so the map never rounds a note onto a cell it is not on. A `primary_unmapped` note (`pattern.py:41`) is placed and drawn like any other. There is one realised map per pattern: mirrors are not patterns but destinations that `schedule_pattern` fans each note to at schedule time, re-resolving the name through each mirror's own map and dropping a voice the mirror lacks (`sequencer.py:1001-1041`), so the map says what the pattern offers its destinations, not what each device voices. The map is sent in full (128 cells is under 1 KB and 12 us to encode, #1920) rather than as a diff, so an edit made behind the helper's back (a REPL poking the dict, an OSC `/data` replacement) is still reflected once per cycle. Presence is all the v1 map carries; velocity per realised cell is a later field.

## The confirmation carrier

| | A. Per-cycle `cycle` event: `built_ver`, `state`, cycle index, start pulse, the full realised map | B. Per-cell build confirmation: a second `changed`, or a `built` flag, for each written cell when the rebuild takes it, as #1916's worked example sketched | C. None: `ack` is the only confirmation and the playhead is the proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frames per cycle per grid | one, under 1 KB for 128 cells (#1920) | one per cell written since the last rebuild; none for untouched cells | none |
| Cells no panel wrote (probability, mute, energy gate, an ephemeral algorithm, a REPL poke) | covered: the map is complete | not covered | not covered |
| Muted or gated pattern | `state` says so and every ring settles to hollow | nothing was taken, so no second `changed` is ever sent: rings never settle | rings never exist |
| What the panel can draw | solid, hollow, ghost, plus the ring | solid and the ring | the ring until `ack`, then nothing |
| Cost | a map per pattern per cycle; a diff on the panel | per-cell bookkeeping on the server of which writes were built | nothing |

**Short list: all three. Recommendation: A.** The full map is the price of showing what will actually sound, which the specification's "a cell shows when it is being played" and the duet both need; B cannot express gating and fails under mute for the same reason the `apply()` stamp alone failed; C leaves the panel unable to tell a tap that will sound from one that will not.

## Conflict policy between writers

The candidates are the three register semantics of the CRDT literature applied to a single authority. Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero and Zawirski, RR-7506 §3.2.1, define the Last-Writer-Wins Register as creating "a total order of assignments by associating a timestamp with each update", with timestamps "assumed unique, totally ordered, and consistent with causal order", implementable "as a per-replica counter concatenated with a unique replica identifier"; downstream "the update takes effect only if the new timestamp is greater". §3.2.2 defines the Multi-Value Register that keeps concurrent assignments for a client to reduce later (https://reed.cs.depaul.edu/lperkovic/csc536/lecture10/techreport.pdf). Here the "timestamp" is the order of application on the composition loop, which is unique and total without any clock; the service's `ver` (#1920) is that order made visible.

| | A. Loop-order last writer wins | B. Compare-and-set per cell (a `set` carries the `ver` the panel last saw for the cell; the service refuses with a new `nack` reason `stale` if the cell moved since) | C. Multi-value merge (keep both hands' values, let the panel resolve) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Machinery beyond #1920 | none | a per-cell version on the server, a base version in every `set`, a new `nack` reason, a retry gesture on the panel | version vectors per cell, a resolution UI |
| A stale absolute set (the panel acted on a value that had already changed) | the sender gets what it asked for and every panel sees the change with `by`; harmless because the set is absolute, not a toggle | refused; the sender's cell flashes and the sender taps again to get the value it already asked for | both values shown until someone chooses |
| Two panels, one cell, one cycle (H3) | later human wins; one `cycle` frame settles both | the second panel's set is refused unless it saw the first's `changed` first, which on a LAN it almost always has | both kept |
| Algorithm versus human | the algorithm's write inside the rebuild is last for that cycle whatever the policy, because it carries no base version and runs on the loop | applies only to panels | applies only to panels |
| Re-sent set after a reconnect (#1920), at most 5 s old because the pending timeout has removed anything older | idempotent when it already landed (H6); when it did not, it is applied and wins over an algorithm edit made during the outage, which is what the human who tapped during the Wi-Fi blip expects | refused as `stale` whenever the algorithm moved the cell during the outage: consistent for a document, wrong for an instrument where a tap five seconds ago is still what the player means | n/a |
| Precedent | Ableton Link: "If multiple participants try to change the tempo simultaneously, everyone else will try to follow, but the tempo will be set by the last participant who made the change" (https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/); the Link documentation: "Each participant chooses to adopt the last tempo value that they've seen proposed on the network" (https://ableton.github.io/link/) | databases and REST `If-Match` | Coda, the Amazon shopping cart (RR-7506 §3.2.2) |

**Short list: A and B.** C is excluded: there is one authority, a cell has two values at most for one pulse, and a resolution UI on a step sequencer is absurd. **Recommendation: A.** Absolute sets already remove the one hazard compare-and-set protects against (a replayed relative operation inverting a cell), so B buys a refusal the human then has to override by tapping again, and it turns #1920's reconnect re-send into a stream of `stale` refusals. Rejecting stale writes is the right rule for a document; for an instrument the later hand is right by definition, which is the rule Link chose for tempo. The one case where A lets an older hand win, a replay after an outage, is bounded to 5 s by the timeout and is the hand the player meant.

## Two hands, one cell, one cycle

Because the algorithm writes inside the rebuild, the question is not who wins but what the algorithm's hand is made of: intent that persists, or notes that exist for one cycle. Three shapes, all builder-side, none touching the engine.

| | A. Persistent, unconstrained: the builder writes intent with `grid.set(..., by="algorithm")` and may write any cell | B. Persistent with a yield guard: as A, but the builder skips cells in `touched`, the set of cells a panel wrote since the last rebuild, which the helper fills from panel writes and clears in `apply()` (`grid.set_unless_touched(...)`, or a read of `grid.touched`) | C. Ephemeral: the builder never writes intent; it adds notes beside `grid.apply(p)` with `p.hit_steps` or `p.sequence`, and subtracts with `grid.apply(p, skip=...)`; its hand lives in `pattern.steps` for one cycle and reaches the panel through the realised map as ghost and hollow cells |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Package change | none beyond #1914's helper | one set and one method on the helper | one optional `skip` argument on `apply()` |
| Measured | H1: the cell lights on the tap and goes out at the rebuild with the algorithm's flash, every cycle; the duet is visible and honest | H2: the tap sounds for exactly one cycle, then the algorithm reclaims the cell | H9: intent and `ver` untouched, no `changed`; the realised map carries the hat as ghost cells and kick/4 as a hollow cell |
| What persists | the algorithm's edit is intent: it survives cycles and the human can edit it further | as A | nothing: recomputed every rebuild from intent, cycle and rng, as every other builder verb is |
| A tap on a cell the algorithm also writes | erased at the next rebuild, visibly | kept for one cycle, then erased | kept as intent; drawn hollow while the algorithm masks it, sounding when it stops |
| Wire | a `changed` with `by="algorithm"` per write, plus `cycle` | as A | `cycle` only |
| Fits #1461 (state minimal and late-bound) | the algorithm accumulates state in the grid | as A | yes: a function of its inputs each cycle |
| Fits #1914 (both hands write through the helper) | yes | yes | the algorithm only reads the helper; #1914's reason for both hands writing, that the algorithm's hand appears on the panel, is met by the realised map |
| Suits | an algorithm that edits the score: evolve, mutate, fill in and leave it | as A, politely | an algorithm that decorates: ghost notes, fills, density, probability, masks |
| Cost | a careless algorithm erases taps | one cycle of protection reads as a bug ("it kept my kick for a bar then dropped it") | the human cannot edit what the algorithm plays; an evolving algorithm keeps its own state elsewhere |

**Short list: all three. Recommendation: C as the default idiom, documented on the helper and used by the first use-case's decorating algorithm; A when a composition means an edit to last, with B available to it in one call.** The three coexist in one composition, cost nothing on the wire beyond what the `cycle` event already carries, and the panel draws them without knowing which is in use. Under C the specification's "an algorithm sets or clears steps, the grid shows that immediately" is met at the rebuild, the same moment as under A, as ghost and hollow cells rather than as changed faces; what differs is that the human's intent is never erased. A per-cell owner or lock (a hold gesture setting a reserved cell field `lock` that the helper honours for `by="algorithm"` until cleared; the Elektron-style trig condition of #1916's prior art in reverse) is reserved as a cell field so the wire shape does not change when it arrives, and is not short-listed because it needs a gesture, which is `multi-touch`'s.

## Explicit set, never toggle

#1916 chose `set` and left the door open for a server-side toggle; this point closes it. A toggle is relative, so a duplicate (a re-send after reconnect, a bounced tap) inverts the cell, and a toggle that arrives after the other hand moved the cell inverts the wrong value. A toggle that carries the value the panel saw ("toggle from off") is a set with a base version, which is option B of the conflict table. H6 shows the absolute form absorbing a replay and an equal-value set from another client with no new version and no frame. The panel computes the inverse of its own copy of the server value and sends that as the absolute value; the only cost is that two humans tapping one cell within a millisecond of each other both send the same inverse and the cell ends where both wanted it, which is the right outcome. Stripe's idempotency layer is the same shape for the record: the client generates the key, the server saves "the resulting status code and body of the first request" and returns it on retry, keys may be removed "after they're at least 24 hours old", and the layer "errors if they're not the same" when a key is reused with different parameters (https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests); here the key is (client, seq), the saved body is the `ack`, and the service forgets a client id some minutes after its last socket closes (#1920).

## Version rules

On the service, per app:

- Every `set` is validated at the boundary (#1914's list; `nack` reasons per #1916), forwarded to the app's adapter, marshalled onto the composition loop and applied in arrival order across all clients. A set that changes a value advances `ver` by one and produces one `changed`; a set equal to the current value advances nothing and produces an `ack` with the current `ver` and no `changed` (H6). The equality test is the helper's, on the loop, because only the loop knows the current value.
- The (client, seq) pair is the idempotency key: the service keeps the `ack` it sent for each acknowledged `seq` per client, drops a `set` at or below the highest acknowledged `seq` and repeats that `ack` with its original `ver` (#1920's rule, with the `ver` kept so a replayed write settles against `built_ver` correctly).
- `changed` carries `client` beside `by` when `by` is `panel`, so a panel can tell its own echo from another panel's hand. #1916's `by` vocabulary (`panel`, `algorithm`, `app`) is unchanged; `client` is an additional field.

Inside the composition process, on the loop and off it:

- The helper keeps one ordered outbound log. A marshalled panel write appends its `changed` and then its `ack` at the moment it is applied; an algorithm write inside the builder appends its `changed` inside `set()`; the `pattern_reschedule` listener appends `cycle`. Nothing is emitted from anywhere else. Because every entry is appended on the loop at the moment of application, the log order is the application order, and H8's window cannot invert it: the panel write that lands between a rebuild and its listener is appended after the algorithm's entries from that rebuild, and the `cycle` event that follows carries a `built_ver` below the panel write's `ver`, which the panel rules below handle.
- The loop thread only appends; the adapter's link thread drains the log and does the socket I/O, in log order. #1919 measured socket I/O on the clock loop at p99 1.1 ms and the same link on a daemon thread at p99 0.03 ms, so the drain is woken by a thread-safe hand-off (a `call_soon_threadsafe` onto the link's own loop, or a queue) and the clock loop never touches a socket. The service fans `changed` and `cycle` to every panel and `ack` to its sender, in the order received.

On the panel, per grid:

- Frames are applied in arrival order; one WebSocket is totally ordered (#1920), the log is applied in application order, so a `changed` never arrives before the `ack` of the write that caused it, the `ack` and `changed` for one write carry the same `ver`, `ver` on `changed` never decreases except across a resync, where the snapshot replaces everything.
- A pending map holds each unacknowledged `set` by `seq` with its path, requested value and send time. `ack` moves the entry to armed with its `ver`; `nack` removes it with a failure flash; the 5 s timeout of #1920 removes it and restores the face.
- A `cycle` event with `built_ver` B settles every armed entry on that grid whose ack `ver` is at or below B: if the realised cell equals the intent the cell is solid; if intent is on and realised is off the cell is hollow, and `state` says whether the whole pattern is muted, gated or silent or whether this one cell was gated by probability or an algorithm's mask; if intent is off and realised is on, the algorithm placed a note there and the cell is a ghost. Armed entries with `ver` above B stay armed for the next `cycle`. The same event re-evaluates every cell the panel never touched, so a settled cell can become hollow or ghost and back; that is how probability, mute and an ephemeral algorithm appear.
- A `changed` on a cell that has an armed entry, from a `client` that is not this panel or from the algorithm, with `ver` above the entry's ack `ver`, overtakes it: the face follows, the ring clears, a flash coloured by `by` shows whose hand it was. A foreign `changed` on a cell that is merely pending (no `ack` yet) moves the face and leaves the ring, because this panel's set is still to be applied and will be the later writer.
- A `changed` on a cell with no ring moves the face with a brief flash coloured by `by`; that is how a second panel's taps and a persistent algorithm's edits appear.

## Several panels

Every panel holds one socket to the service (#1919, #1920); the service fans every `changed`, `cycle` and `event` to all of them, so each panel sees the others' taps at the moment they are applied, attributed by `client`. Two panels writing one cell in one cycle (H3) produce two `changed` frames in loop order and one `cycle` frame; the earlier panel is overtaken and sees the later hand's flash, exactly as the algorithm case, because the rule does not distinguish hands. The sender's own echo is recognised by `client` and applied silently. A laptop that opens the same page is a third panel with no special case.

## Reconnect and resync

The resync is #1920's snapshot with three additions inside the grid's `cells` value: `built_ver`, `state` and `realised`, so a panel reconnecting between a rebuild and its cycle start (H5's window, up to one lookahead wide) can draw an intent that will not sound this cycle as armed rather than solid, and a panel reconnecting to a muted pattern draws it hollow. A panel with no `ver` map (fresh tab) and one with a stale map are treated the same: full `manifest` and `snapshot`, then the pending map is replayed in `seq` order (#1920). Every surviving pending entry is re-sent; none is dropped on the panel's judgement, because the 5 s timeout has already removed anything older than 5 s, and because an entry whose write landed before the drop is answered by the dedup with its original `ack` and `ver` (H6), which is what lets the panel settle it against the snapshot's `built_ver`. An entry whose write did not land is applied now and wins, which is what the human who tapped during the Wi-Fi blip expects; the algorithm's hand, if it moved the cell meanwhile, returns at its next write or its next cycle.

## What a pending tap looks like on the cell

| | A. The face is the server's value; a ring is the human's request | B. Optimistic face: the face flips on the tap and reverts on `nack` or timeout; a ring marks armed | C. Optimistic face, no marks: the face flips on the tap; `changed` and `cycle` correct it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tap to something visible | the ring, on the tap itself | the face, on the tap | the face, on the tap |
| Face on a wired LAN | follows on `ack`, a few milliseconds later | at once | at once |
| Face on a WAN (15 to 100 ms round-trip, #1920) | ring alone for the round-trip; inside Nielsen's 0.1 s at 15 ms, over it at 100 ms | at once | at once |
| Two panels agree on every face | always | within one round-trip | within one round-trip |
| Refusal (`nack`, timeout) | ring off and a flash; the face never moved | the face reverts: a flicker | the face reverts: a flicker |
| Sent, landed and built distinguishable | yes: dim ring, bright ring, none | landed and built by the ring | no |
| Precedent | Launchpad Pro MK3 Programmer Mode: "every pad and button sends out a MIDI message and may be lit with the same message", and a Custom Mode may have "no On Colour (ie pads will not light when triggered from the hardware itself)" so that "pads may be lit via MIDI messages" (https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/s3fs-public/downloads/LPP3_prog_ref_guide_200415.pdf, Programmer Mode and Live Mode + Custom sections): the host owns the face | most software step sequencers | a plain toggle |

**Short list: all three. Recommendation: A.** Nothing the panel draws on a face is ever speculative, so two panels always agree on the faces and the ring is private to the panel that tapped; the ring appears on the tap and the face follows on `ack` within a few milliseconds on a LAN, both inside Nielsen's 0.1 s threshold for a system that feels instantaneous (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/). B is the fallback if the ring proves illegible on the panel (Hardware-gated) or when a WAN mode arrives, where #1920 notes the acknowledgement should not wait for the `ack`; the two differ only in how the interval between tap and `ack` is drawn, and no version rule changes. Push 2 gives the host the same control over colour by velocity and animation by channel (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ableton/push-interface/master/doc/AbletonPush2MIDIDisplayInterface.asc) but does not state whether pads light locally, so it is not relied on. Three visual layers suffice, as #1916 concluded from the Launchpad's static, flashing and pulsing: face (off, solid, hollow, ghost), ring (none, pending dim, armed bright), and overlay (playing, flash by hand). How the layers are coloured is `ui-rendering`'s; how long the armed ring lasts is H4's column, from under a pulse to one cycle after the tap with a one-pulse lookahead (42 ms to 2.02 s at 120 BPM on a one-bar pattern), proportional to how early in the cycle the tap fell, which is itself information the player reads.

## Additions and revisions this point asks of the neighbouring contracts

- To #1914's helper: `built_ver`, `built_cycle` and an `applied` flag stamped in `apply()`; a `pre_ver` recorded by the adapter at `reschedule_pulse`; a `touched` set of cells written by a panel since the last rebuild, cleared in `apply()`, with a `set_unless_touched()` convenience; an optional `skip` argument on `apply()` for the ephemeral idiom; the single ordered outbound log with `ver`, `by` and `client` that the link thread drains; a reserved cell field `lock`. One candidate for #1914's small engine changes: a `last_build_failed` flag on the running pattern, so a builder that raised after `apply()` can be reported as `state: failed` rather than shown as an empty cycle; and public read access to `_muted` and `_energy_gated`, which #1914 already lists for the private names the adapter reads.
- To #1916's contract, as a revision of its semantics rather than an addition: `changed` for a `next_cycle` leaf leaves at application, in the same pulse as `ack` and with the same `ver`, and the new `cycle` event is the build confirmation. #1916's worked example (`ack` at ver 1207, then `changed` at ver 1212 "when the rebuild has taken it") becomes `ack` and `changed` both at ver 1207 followed by `cycle` with `built_ver` at or above 1207, and its `applies` rule ("light it and mark it pending until `changed` confirms") reads "until `cycle` confirms" for grids. `applies` keeps its meaning of when a write lands. As additions: `client` on `changed` when `by` is `panel`; a grid `event` named `cycle` with `built_ver`, `state`, `cycle`, `start_pulse`, `realised` and `off_grid`; `built_ver`, `state` and `realised` inside the grid's `cells` value in `snapshot`.
- To #1920's transport: the dedup keeps the `ver` of each acknowledged `seq`, so a repeated `ack` carries its original `ver`; the replay after a snapshot re-sends every surviving pending entry with no further filtering. No frame changes.
- To #1919's placement: the frame log is appended on the composition loop and drained by the link thread; no socket I/O on the loop.

Nothing here needs an engine change: the commit point, the ordering, the confirmation hook and the mute and gate returns exist today, and the algorithm's precedence is a fact of `_rebuild()` rather than a policy.

## Not covered

A builder that raised after calling `apply()` leaves `steps` empty (`composition.py:6094-6101`) and is indistinguishable in the `cycle` event from a builder that placed nothing; the panel draws every intent-on cell hollow, which is the honest picture for a silent cycle, and naming the cause needs the `last_build_failed` flag above. An audition of a freshly set cell in the current cycle through `composition.trigger()` (`composition.py:5224-5250`; `_schedule_one_shot` is thread-safe, `composition.py:5383-5400`), which #1914 lists as possible, would add a fourth moment (heard once before its cycle) and a state to the diagram and is not designed here. The per-cell lock is reserved as a field and not designed; its gesture is `multi-touch`'s. Flash durations, ring weights and the colours of hollow and ghost faces are `ui-rendering`'s. The pending timeout is inherited from #1920 and was not re-derived for Wi-Fi. Velocity in the realised map is deferred. The dedup was modelled in the helper (H6) rather than in the service where #1920 places it; the semantics are the same and the placement was not prototyped end to end.

## Hardware-gated

Whether a dim ring, a bright ring, a hollow face and a ghost face are distinguishable at arm's length under studio lighting on the iiyama panel decides between options A and B for the pending look; the version rules do not change with the answer, only the drawing. Whether the 5 s pending timeout suits the panel host's Wi-Fi drop behaviour is #1920's gated check and applies here unchanged. The end-to-end tap-to-`ack` figure across a real LAN segment (browser to service to app and back) needs a second host to run the service on, as #1920 records.
