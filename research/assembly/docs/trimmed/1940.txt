#1940  Every short-listed library, font and icon set is AGPL-3.0-compatible and vendorable with its notice kept; the licence exclusions are the two GPL-3.0 widget sources and Web Awesome's proprietary Pro tier, and NexusUI, Interface.js and Faust UI carry a licence field with no licence text
  finding · active · #licence-audit · #research · superintendent

Summary: Every item recommended or short-listed by the ui-rendering, serving and transport-ui points was checked at its primary source on 2026-09-03 and is under a licence the FSF classes as GPL-compatible and its FAQ extends to AGPLv3 (MIT, BSD-3, ISC, Apache-2.0 one way, Unlicense, PSF-2.0, LGPL-2.1 and LGPL-3.0), or is an OFL-1.1 font aggregated under its own licence. Open Stage Control (GPL-3.0) and Faust UI (GPL-3.0-or-later) are combinable only as separate GPL-3.0 modules under section 13 of both licences and cannot be relicensed, so they stay prior art; Web Awesome is MIT only in its Core package, and its Pro components (data grid, charts, combobox, date picker) are sold under a licence that forbids redistribution. NexusUI and Interface.js state MIT only in `package.json` or a README sentence with no licence file, which leaves nothing to retain under MIT's notice condition, and Faust UI states GPL-3.0-or-later in `package.json` with no copy of the GPL, which GPL-3.0 section 4 requires be given to every recipient. The binding obligations are notice and source obligations on the vendored browser assets: the minified Preact+htm bundle carries no notice, Supervisor's vendored Gridstack lost its LICENSE.txt, each minified vendored file needs its upstream version and source location recorded so the Corresponding Source can be supplied, the fonts ship as OFL works with their copyright line and the OFL text (unmodified upstream WOFF2 by choice; subsetting is permitted for Inter and JetBrains Mono, which reserve no name, and a subset of IBM Plex may not be called "Plex"), and Lucide needs both its ISC and Feather MIT notices. Python dependencies are declared, not vendored, so nothing third-party from PyPI enters the tree.

**Question.** Is every library, font, icon set and widget source named by the ui-rendering, serving and transport-ui points compatible with AGPL-3.0, may it be vendored, and what attribution does each carry?

## How compatibility was decided

The project licence is AGPL-3.0. The three sibling apps declare it as the PEP 639 expression `AGPL-3.0-or-later` (`/mnt/dev/Apps/2026-02 Sequencer/pyproject.toml:14`, `/mnt/dev/Apps/Subsample/pyproject.toml:14`, `/mnt/dev/Apps/SDR Scanner/pyproject.toml:18`), each with the AGPL text as `LICENSE` at the repository root; Supervisor declares `AGPL-3.0-only` (`/mnt/dev/Apps/Supervisor/pyproject.toml:9`) and has no `LICENSE` file at its root (the root holds `client`, `mocks`, `pyproject.toml`, `README.md`, `serve.py`, `supervisor`, `supervisor.egg-info`, `tests`). The rulings below hold for either expression, because the difference between AGPLv3 and GPLv3 is section 13 alone and the FSF's compatibility statements are made for both: "Each place that the matrix states GPLv3, the same statement about compatibility is true for AGPLv3 as well" (https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#AllCompatibility). The classification of each licence family is the FSF's (https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.en.html, read on 2026-09-03 with `curl` because the host refused the fetch tool; the saved copy is `license-list.html` beside this document's notes), checked against the licence texts at SPDX:

- Expat (MIT), modified BSD (3-clause), ISC: "compatible with the GNU GPL" (license-list, `#Expat`, `#ModifiedBSD`, `#ISC`). Obligation from the texts: keep the copyright notice and the permission notice in all copies (https://spdx.org/licenses/MIT.html, https://spdx.org/licenses/ISC.html); BSD-3 additionally requires the notice, conditions and disclaimer in binary redistributions' documentation and forbids using the holder's name to endorse (https://spdx.org/licenses/BSD-3-Clause.html).
- Apache-2.0: "compatible with version 3 of the GNU GPL. Please note that this license is not compatible with GPL version 2" (license-list, `#apache2`); the Apache Software Foundation says the same one way, "Apache 2 software can therefore be included in GPLv3 projects" while "GPLv3 software cannot be included in Apache projects" (https://www.apache.org/licenses/GPL-compatibility.html). Its section 4 requires a copy of the licence with any redistribution, prominent notices on modified files, retention of copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices, and reproduction of any `NOTICE` file's attribution notices (https://spdx.org/licenses/Apache-2.0.html). Apache's patent-termination and notice terms are the kind of additional term AGPLv3 section 7 admits (https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html).
- The Unlicense: "a public domain dedication ... Both public domain works and the lax license provided by the Unlicense are compatible with the GNU GPL" (`#Unlicense`; text at https://spdx.org/licenses/Unlicense.html). No notice obligation.
- PSF-2.0 (Python 2.0.1 and newer): "a free software license and is compatible with the GNU GPL" (`#Python`). Obligations: retain the PSF copyright notice, and a derivative must carry "a brief summary of the changes made to Python" (https://spdx.org/licenses/PSF-2.0.html); an unmodified dependency triggers neither beyond the notice.
- LGPL-2.1: "compatible with GPLv2 and GPLv3" (`#LGPLv2.1`); the mechanism is its section 3, which lets a copy be placed under "the ordinary GNU General Public License, version 2" or "a newer version" (https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-2.1-only.html). LGPL-3.0: "compatible with GPLv3" (`#LGPL`). LGPL-3.0 "incorporates the terms and conditions of version 3 of the GNU General Public License, supplemented by the additional permissions listed below" (its section 0; its own sections 0 to 6 never name the AGPL, https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-3.0-only.html), so it reaches the AGPL through GPL-3.0 section 13, which permits combining "any covered work with a work licensed under version 3 of the GNU Affero General Public License into a single combined work" (https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html; the SPDX LGPL-3.0 page also shows the sentence because it appends the full GPL-3.0 text). Either way the library stays LGPL and its source must remain available; under an AGPL project that publishes everything this is met by shipping or pointing to the library's unminified source.
- GPL-3.0: the AGPL "is also technically not compatible with GPLv3 in a strict sense: you cannot take code released under the GNU AGPL and convey or modify it however you like under the terms of GPLv3, or vice versa. However, you are allowed to combine separate modules or source files released under both of those licenses in a single project" (`#AGPLv3.0`); "You can always link GPLv3-covered modules with AGPLv3-covered modules, and vice versa" (gpl-faq `#AGPLGPL`); the permissions are GPL-3.0 section 13 and AGPL-3.0 section 13 (https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html, https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html). A GPL-3.0 file therefore cannot be merged into an AGPL-3.0 file or relicensed; it can only sit beside the project as a GPL-3.0 module, which is outside the decision's wording "redistributable under the project licence". Conveying such a module also requires that the conveyor "give all recipients a copy of this License along with the Program" (GPL-3.0 section 4).
- OFL-1.1: "a free copyleft license for fonts. Its only unusual requirement is that when selling the font, you must redistribute it bundled with some software, rather than alone" (`#SILOFL`). It is not a code licence and the fonts are aggregated, not merged: "Fonts licensed under the OFL can be freely included alongside other software under FLOSS (Free/Libre and Open Source Software) licenses" and serving through `@font-face` is distribution (https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/). Its conditions (https://spdx.org/licenses/OFL-1.1.html): "Original or Modified Versions of the Font Software may be bundled, redistributed and/or sold with any software, provided that each copy contains the above copyright notice and this license" (condition 2); "No Modified Version of the Font Software may use the Reserved Font Name(s)" and "This restriction only applies to the primary font name as presented to the users" (condition 3); the font "must be distributed entirely under this license, and must not be distributed under any other license" (condition 5). Subsetting, changing format or "Removing any parts of the font when delivering a webfont to a browser" is modification, and the FAQ's own ruling on it is "This is permitted by the OFL but would not normally allow the use of RFNs" (OFL FAQ 2.6). So the fonts stay OFL inside the AGPL tree; a subset of Inter or JetBrains Mono, which declare no Reserved Font Name, is compliant under its own name with the copyright line and OFL text kept; a subset of IBM Plex may not be called "Plex". Vendoring the upstream WOFF2 files unmodified is the simpler path (nothing to rename, nothing to document as changed) and is what #1931 recommends, but it is a choice, not a licence obligation; #1931's fonts section reads as if the OFL required it, which this audit corrects, and subsetting stays open as a load-time lever on a Raspberry Pi host.

## What the table shows

- Every item on any short list or recommendation (Preact, htm, Lit, Inter, JetBrains Mono, IBM Plex, Lucide, Tabler, Phosphor, wavesurfer.js, Gridstack, Web Awesome Core, webaudio-controls, Starlette, uvicorn, websockets and their dependencies, PyYAML, python-osc, pydantic, aiohttp) is under a licence the FSF classes as GPL-compatible and that its FAQ extends to AGPLv3, or is an OFL font aggregated under its own licence. No candidate is MPL, CDDL, EPL, CC or proprietary; MPL-2.0 appears only in tools of the research venvs (mypy's `pathspec`, `certifi`), not in anything proposed for the service.
- The exclusions on licence grounds are Open Stage Control (GPL-3.0) and Faust UI (GPL-3.0-or-later), lawful beside an AGPL work as separate modules, not admissible under the decision that borrowed material be redistributable under the project licence, and not relicensable, so they are prior art for conventions; and Web Awesome's Pro components, which are proprietary and non-redistributable under a brand that also covers the MIT Core package, so the fallback "if dialogs multiply" is Core from npm and nothing from the Pro catalogue.
- Three candidates carry a licence field but no licence text: NexusUI and Faust UI in `package.json`, Interface.js in one README sentence. A `package.json` `license` value is a statement by the author that npm and GitHub both read as the licence. For the two MIT claims the defect is that MIT's own condition is that "the above copyright notice and this permission notice shall be included in all copies", and there is no notice above anything; for Faust UI it is that GPL-3.0 section 4 requires a copy of the GPL with every conveyed copy and the package carries none. Vendoring NexusUI or Interface.js means writing the notice from the repository's author field and year, and asking upstream for a `LICENSE` file is the clean remedy. NexusUI is the only one of the three on a short list, as a fallback.
- Two version discrepancies against the ui-rendering design #1931, neither of which changes a ruling: it cites NexusUI as 2.2.0, which is the `package.json` on master, while the npm `latest` tag is 2.1.6, so a vendored copy from npm is one minor version behind the repository (both MIT); and it cites `@vaadin/button` as 24.9.18 where the registry returned 25.2.10 on 2026-09-03 (both Apache-2.0).
- peaks.js's peer range excludes the current konva (10.3.2 against `< 10`), which #1931 did not record; it is excluded anyway.
- The obligations that actually bind the tree are notice and source obligations on the browser assets, because they are the only third-party files conveyed. The minified Preact+htm bundle carries no notice of its own, and Supervisor's vendored Gridstack lost its `LICENSE.txt` on the way in (`client/lib/gridstack-all.js:1` names a file that is not in `client/lib/`). The consequence for Superintendent's static directory is one licence file per vendored asset beside it (or one `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES` file with each notice in full), the fonts with their copyright line and OFL text, and the Lucide directory with both its ISC and Feather MIT notices; Subsequence's `assets/web/*.html` package-data line (`/mnt/dev/Apps/2026-02 Sequencer/pyproject.toml:53-54`) is the shape that ships them in the wheel, widened to cover the licence files.
- Corresponding Source. AGPL-3.0 section 1 defines it as "all the source code needed to generate, install, and (for an executable work) run the object code and to modify the work", excluding only System Libraries and "general-purpose tools or generally available free programs which are used unmodified in performing those activities but which are not part of the work" (https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html). A minified `standalone.module.js`, `lit-all.min.js` or `gridstack-all.js` inside the wheel is part of the conveyed work on the conservative reading, so whoever conveys Superintendent must be able to supply or point to the unminified Preact, htm, Lit or Gridstack source at the vendored version. The same one-line habit the LGPL rows already carry belongs on these: record the exact upstream version and its source URL beside each minified vendored file (a `VERSIONS` or `SOURCES` entry next to the licence file), or vendor the unminified module instead.
- Apache-2.0 assets (htm now, webaudio-controls if adopted) must be vendored unmodified or carry a change notice, and any `NOTICE` file must be reproduced; neither has one.
- Fonts: the OFL permits subsetting and re-encoding for Inter and JetBrains Mono under their own names, and forbids only the name "Plex" on a modified IBM Plex. Vendoring the upstream WOFF2 unmodified is the recommended default because it needs no renaming and no change record, and subsetting remains available if a Raspberry Pi host's font load time turns out to matter.
- Superintendent's own licence carries one clause worth recording beside the others. AGPL section 13 opens "if you modify the Program, your modified version must prominently offer all users interacting with it remotely through a computer network ... an opportunity to receive the Corresponding Source" (https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html); it binds whoever modifies and runs or conveys the program, not the copyright holder conveying his own work. A source link in the page footer or the manifest costs nothing, is customary for AGPL network software, and makes compliance automatic for anyone who forks and runs it.
- Where this audit corrects #1931: the fonts section's "unmodified and unsubsetted" is a recommendation, not an OFL requirement; the Web Awesome row must exclude the Pro tier; and the Vaadin version is 25.2.10.
