#1928  Each app carries its own adapter module on a daemon thread that dials the service over one WebSocket and speaks the browser's frame vocabulary; inbound sets queue and land at the sequencer's own hooks, OSC stays the user door and MIDI the pad path
  design · active · #research · #transport-apps · superintendent

Summary: Four candidates for the service-to-app link were evaluated against the three concurrency models: OSC over UDP, Supervisor's embedded WebSocket server, an in-process adapter that dials the service (over WebSocket or over stdlib TCP JSON lines), and MIDI into Subsample's virtual port for pads. Recommended: one adapter module per app following a documented shape, running on its own daemon thread and loop, opening a single outbound WebSocket to the service, sending its `manifest` and `snapshot` on connect in the vocabulary of #1916, and applying inbound `set` and `fire` frames through each app's own cross-thread rule (a bounded queue drained in the `reschedule_pulse` and `beat` listeners in Subsequence, locked reads and MIDI into the virtual port in Subsample, `call_soon_threadsafe` onto the scanner loop in Substation). Measured on the real clock, the recommended shape costs Subsequence nothing (p99 0.002 ms drained at `reschedule_pulse` and `beat`, against a 0.002 ms baseline) where every shape that wakes the clock loop per message costs about 1.1 ms at p99. The alternatives kept are the same adapter over stdlib TCP and Supervisor's embedded-server shape behind #1919's reverse proxy; OSC is excluded as the standard shape because it has no connection to hang a snapshot on, cannot see an absent peer, and would put Superintendent behind the LAN-open user door; MIDI is the pad complement, carried as a `fire` on a declared trigger, not a candidate. A per-app module with a protocol specification and executable test vectors is preferred over a shared package for v1, with extraction into a package once the three modules exist and converge.

**Question.** How does the Superintendent service reach each music app, in one shape that applies to Subsequence, Subsample and Substation without touching their hot paths?

## Summary

Four candidates were evaluated against the three concurrency models (the constraints are in the companion finding): OSC over UDP, which every app already carries; a WebSocket server embedded in each app, Supervisor's shape; a small in-process adapter that dials the service and speaks its wire envelope; and MIDI into Subsample's virtual port for pads. Recommended: a per-app adapter module, one file in each package following one documented shape, that runs on its own daemon thread with its own asyncio loop, dials the service over a single outbound WebSocket, sends its `manifest` and `snapshot` on connect, and hands inbound commands to the app through that app's own cross-thread rule: a bounded queue drained in the `reschedule_pulse` and `beat` listeners in Subsequence, direct calls under the documented locks or MIDI into the virtual port in Subsample, `call_soon_threadsafe` onto the scanner loop in Substation. Measured on the real clock, this shape costs Subsequence nothing (p99 0.002 ms against a 0.002 ms baseline in the same block) where every shape that wakes the clock loop per message costs about 1.1 ms at p99. The alternatives kept are the same adapter over a stdlib TCP line protocol, and Supervisor's embedded-server shape behind the reverse proxy of #1919. OSC is excluded as the standard shape and stays the user-facing door; MIDI is the pad complement, not a candidate.

## What the code establishes

- Subsequence's socket surfaces on the clock loop are the OSC server (`subsequence/osc.py:82-88`), the REPL (`subsequence/live_server.py:54-58`) and the legacy WebSocket (`subsequence/web_ui.py:164`), all started in `_run()` (`subsequence/composition.py:5861-5883`); the legacy HTTP server is the one network surface already on a daemon thread (`web_ui.py:117-129`). Cross-thread entry is by marshalling onto `_sequencer._event_loop` (`_schedule_one_shot`, `composition.py:5383-5400`; `subsequence/live_reloader.py:246-255`). `composition.data` (`composition.py:1454`, handed to every builder at `composition.py:6055`), `tweak()` (`composition.py:4129-4151`) and `mute()` (`composition.py:3917-3938`) are the write paths a control message lands on; they are read at the next rebuild. The events an adapter can subscribe to are `start`, `stop`, `bar`, `beat`, `reschedule_pulse`, `pattern_reschedule`, `section` (`composition.py:2462-2468`; emitted at `subsequence/sequencer.py:1416`, `1427`, `1804`, `1829`).
- Subsample is threads with no loop (`subsample/cli.py:1726-1810`); its Supervisor adapter was created before the worker threads and run with `start_threaded()` (`cli.py:1747-1759`), and its events arrive on recorder, worker and rtmidi threads (`subsample/events.py:7`, `subsample/player.py:5055`). Its only trigger path is MIDI into the virtual input port (`player.py:4444-4450`); the OSC receiver accepts only `/sample/import` (`subsample/osc.py:152`).
- Substation has no inbound path at all and its OSC sender is neither imported by the CLI nor configured by default (`substation/osc_sender.py:16-20`; `substation/config.yaml.default` has no `osc` section). Its Supervisor adapter was awaited on the scanner's loop before `scan()` (`substation/cli.py:157-167`, `240-252`); `recording_started` and `channel_state` reach handlers through `loop=` marshalling from the executor thread (`substation/scanner.py:1438`, `1892`), `recording_saved` and `recording_discarded` are emitted from a coroutine already on the loop (`scanner.py:1458`, `1499-1521`, handed over at `1874-1876`), and the per-slice `noise_floor` and `channel_snr` run on the executor thread (`scanner.py:1617`, `1899`; dispatch rules at `402-439`).
- Supervisor's `BroadcastServer` is the working precedent for an in-process adapter shape: manifest on connect, dirty-flag throttle, heartbeat, `start_threaded()` for a threaded host (`supervisor/core.py:23-119`, `174-257`); the class touches no host object, and it is the app halves that reach into private state (`supervisor/app/subsample.py:154-166`). It was consumed as a shared package pulled from git by optional extras (`subsample/pyproject.toml` `[supervisor]`; `substation/cli.py:240-252`).
- #1919 (topology) has already settled that the browser holds one socket to one service, that the apps dial the service and declare themselves on connect, and that socket I/O on the clock loop costs the tail about a millisecond whether it is one link or a fan-out server. #1920 (transport-ui) has chosen one WebSocket per page carrying JSON envelopes in #1916's vocabulary with a `manifest` and `snapshot` on connect and absolute sets. #1914 (subsequence-grid) puts the grid in `composition.data` behind a `StepGrid` helper. This point decides the app side of the same link and reconciles it with #1914 below.

## The candidates

**A. OSC over UDP.** python-osc is already a hard dependency of Subsequence (`pyproject.toml`), an optional extra in Subsample and Substation, Unlicense (https://pypi.org/project/python-osc/, 1.10.2, Python 3.10 or later). Subsequence's receiver runs on the clock loop with synchronous handlers (`osc.py:82-88`; python-osc's asyncio server calls `call_handlers_for_packet` from `datagram_received`, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/attwad/python-osc/master/pythonosc/osc_server.py); Subsample's is a `ThreadingOSCUDPServer` on a daemon thread (`subsample/osc.py:155-175`); Substation would need a receiver written. Bidirectional only as two independent one-way streams, each side needing the other's address and a port of its own, so two more ports per app on the port map. No connection, so no connect event to hang a snapshot on and no way to know the peer is absent: UDP "delivery and duplicate protection are not guaranteed" (https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc768). The type set is int32, float32, string and blob with T, F, N and I optional (https://opensoundcontrol.stanford.edu/spec-1_0.html); python-osc's message builder maps a Python `None` to the Nil type tag (`osc_message_builder.py:140-141` in the installed 1.10.2; its CHANGELOG records "Support for sending nil values" at 1.7.4 and "Add support for arguments with Nil type" at 1.8.1, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/attwad/python-osc/master/CHANGELOG.md), and Substation's sender still encodes "no tone" as zero because "OSC has no native null" (`substation/osc_sender.py:28-30`; #1478). A control declaration (#1916 is a typed tree) or a grid snapshot does not fit OSC arguments and would ride as a JSON string inside an OSC string in any case, at which point the protocol is JSON with an OSC header. Clock cost measured: on the loop 1.14 ms p99 at 50 Hz; on a thread with per-datagram `call_soon_threadsafe` 1.16 ms; on a thread with a per-beat drain 0.008 ms. Service absent: sends vanish with no error, which is harmless to the app and invisible to it. Reusing Subsequence's existing receiver would also merge Superintendent into the user-facing OSC vocabulary on `0.0.0.0:9000`, the control door recorded in #1922.

**B. A WebSocket server embedded in each app, Supervisor's shape.** `websockets` (BSD-3-Clause, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/python-websockets/websockets/main/LICENSE) is a Subsequence dependency and would become one for Subsample and Substation. The new asyncio implementation arrived in 13.0 and the legacy one is deprecated since 14.0 (https://websockets.readthedocs.io/en/stable/project/changelog.html); Subsequence declares `websockets>=12.0` (`pyproject.toml:23`) while `web_ui.py:21` imports `websockets.asyncio.server`, so the floor must rise to 13.0 whatever is chosen (#1920 pins the service at 14). Bidirectional and connection-oriented: manifest and snapshot on connect are natural (`core.py:174-189`). Thread affinity: a loop per app on a daemon thread (`start_threaded()`), which #1919 measured at nothing on the clock (p99 0.003 ms) against 1.1 ms on the loop; in Substation the server could sit on the scanner loop as Supervisor's did, but the one shape applied to each app puts it on a thread there too. Each app gains a listening socket and a port (Supervisor used 9003 and 9004), the service holds static configuration of every app's address, and fan-out to every browser happens inside each app unless a middle tier is in front; #1919 keeps this alive only behind a reverse proxy. Service absent: nothing happens; the app serves nobody. App absent: the service retries.

**C. An in-process adapter that dials the service.** The registration direction #1919 recommends: each app holds one address, opens one outbound connection, sends its `manifest` (#1916) and `snapshot`, then exchanges the same frame types the browser uses (#1920) so the service has one codec for app and browser frames. No listening socket in any app, so the port map and the exposure in #1922 do not grow. Two wire options were measured:

- **C-ws, a WebSocket client.** `websockets.asyncio.client` on a daemon thread with its own loop in all three apps (in Substation it could share the scanner loop, which is not a clock, but one shape argues for the thread everywhere). Framing, close handshake and keepalive come with the library: it sends a ping every 20 s by default and closes the connection when no pong arrives within 20 s (https://websockets.readthedocs.io/en/stable/topics/keepalive.html), so a dead service is noticed without hand-rolled heartbeats. Adds `websockets` to Subsample and Substation as an optional extra, import-guarded like Supervisor's hook (`cli.py:1747-1759`, `substation/cli.py:240-252`). Clock cost: on the loop 1.22 ms p99 and 2.74 ms max at 50 Hz; on a thread with per-message `call_soon_threadsafe` 1.10 ms p99 and 1.61 ms max (repeated in the second block at 1.19 ms p99), the same tail as every other per-message shape; on a thread with the inbound queue drained in the `beat` listener 0.002 ms p99 and 0.020 ms max, drained in the `reschedule_pulse` and `beat` listeners with a real repeating pattern 0.002 ms p99 and 1.108 ms max (a single late pulse while other work raised the load average to 1.55; the heavier drain of about a hundred writes at `reschedule_pulse` alone had a maximum of 0.019 ms), against baselines of 0.002 and 0.003 ms in the same block; under a `SelectSelector` loop 0.006 ms p99 even per message.
- **C-tcp, a TCP client speaking newline-delimited JSON.** Standard library only (`socket`, `threading`, `json`), one reader thread and one writer thread, no new dependency anywhere. The service must implement a second framing and keepalive beside its WebSocket one, and every envelope detail (ping, close, size limits) is hand-rolled. Clock cost with per-message `call_soon_threadsafe`: 1.05 ms p99 and 1.17 ms max, the same class of tail; with a drain it would be the baseline, since the drain is the app-side half and is identical to the measured C-ws drain.

Service absent, for either: the adapter logs once, retries with capped backoff, and the app runs exactly as it does today; a service that dies mid-session drops the link, the adapter's sends fail into a closed socket and are dropped, and the next connect re-sends `manifest` and `snapshot`, so nothing is stale for longer than the reconnect.

**D. MIDI into Subsample's virtual port, for pads only.** Subsample's input is `mido.open_input(name, virtual=True)` (`player.py:4444-4450`); a co-located process opens an output to that name exactly as Subsequence does (#611). Dispatch is under 1 ms on the rtmidi thread (`player.py:7-12`). Not bidirectional and no snapshot: it is a trigger path, not a control link. Virtual ports "are not available under Microsoft Windows" (https://mido.readthedocs.io/en/stable/backends/rtmidi.html; the same in python-rtmidi's documentation, https://spotlightkid.github.io/python-rtmidi/rtmidi.html), so it holds for the Linux server and would need loopMIDI on a Windows host. Across hosts it becomes RTP-MIDI (RFC 6295, which "defines tools for graceful recovery from packet loss", https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6295); on Linux `rtpmidid` shares "ALSA sequencer devices on the network using RTP MIDI", is GPLv3 with an LGPL 2.1 library, notes that "On MacOS there is support included in the OS" and lists Tobias Erichsen's rtpmidi for Windows among related implementations, without an explicit interoperability statement about either (https://github.com/davidmoreno/rtpmidid). Failure: sends into an absent port fail at open, and a sender that dies between a pad's note-on and its note-off leaves a `gated` or `loop` voice sounding until a later note-off or CC 123 on that channel, while a `one_shot` voice is unaffected (`player.py:4967-4972`, `5057-5095`, `3483-3485`); whoever sends the press must own the release, and the service must send the release (or CC 123 for the channel) when the panel that is holding a pad disconnects. D is a complement the adapter can carry as one control type, not a shape for the three apps; how it is carried is settled under the pad path below.

## Where the adapter lives, given #1465

#1465 sorts values, not verbs: a drum map or a channel constant belongs in the composition file; "MIDI output abstraction" and "timing and scheduling" belong in the module. An adapter that exposes generic control (declare what this composition can do, set a named value, report events) is the same kind of thing as `osc.py`, `live_server.py` and `web_ui.py`, all of which sit in the package and none of which knows a studio. So the Subsequence adapter is an engine module beside them, enabled by an opt-in call like `composition.superintendent(url=...)` in the same pattern as `composition.osc()` and `composition.live()`; nothing it carries may name a device, a channel or a voice. Whatever the grid helper of #1914 exposes, the adapter forwards by name. In Subsample and Substation the equivalent is one module each (`subsample/superintendent.py`, `substation/superintendent.py`), wired in the CLI where the Supervisor hooks are today (`cli.py:1747-1759`, `substation/cli.py:157-167`), enabled by configuration and import-guarded.

## Reconciliation with #1914: who owns the thread-to-loop crossing for grid writes

#1914 describes the `StepGrid` helper as marshalling writes onto the loop with `call_soon_threadsafe` per write. The finding measures exactly that call at about 1.1 ms p99 on the clock when it arrives per message from another thread, and measures a queue drained by a listener already on the loop at nothing. The two documents are reconciled by one rule: the adapter thread never calls the helper. Inbound `set` frames go into the adapter's bounded queue; the adapter's `reschedule_pulse` listener (and its `beat` listener, for patterns not rescheduling this pulse) drains the queue on the loop and calls the helper's write from there. For that to cost nothing the helper's write must apply directly when it is already on the loop and marshal only when it is not, which is the probe `_schedule_one_shot` already uses (`asyncio.get_running_loop()` raising `RuntimeError` off the loop, `composition.py:5386-5395`). So the crossing for panel writes is owned by the adapter's queue and drain; the helper's per-write marshalling stays as the safety net for any other off-loop writer (a REPL thread, a user script) and costs that writer the measured wake-up, which is acceptable for a human at a keyboard and not for a panel at fifty taps a second. #1914 needs the one sentence about the on-loop probe added to its helper description; this document states the rule, and the assembler should carry it into #1914 so it is stated once in both.

## The pad path: which process sends the MIDI

#1916 declares a pad as a `trigger` driven by the `fire` verb, with a `midi` block the service "uses to send to the virtual port when co-located"; candidate D above is that path. The recommended adapter can also carry `fire` into Subsample and send the MIDI itself. The two are not the same design and the difference was not weighed by the researcher; it is weighed here and the choice is handed to `subsample-substation`, which owns pad triggering and has `latency`'s numbers.

| | Service opens a mido output to the virtual port (#1916's `midi` block, candidate D) | Subsample adapter module receives `fire` over the link and opens a mido output into its own process's virtual port | Subsample adapter calls a new public `MidiPlayer` method from its thread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hops from the service to the rtmidi thread | ALSA only | one WebSocket frame on loopback (about 1 ms, the order #1920 measured for a browser round trip), one thread handoff, then ALSA | one WebSocket frame, then a direct call |
| Works when the service is not on the sampler's host | no; needs RTP-MIDI | yes | yes |
| Subsample's lock model | untouched | untouched: the input callback thread stays the only MIDI handler | `_handle_message` from a second thread; not covered by the docstring's model (`player.py:7-29`) and unreviewed |
| Where `fire` is consumed | in the service; Subsample never sees it | in the adapter, as one more frame type of the one codec | as the middle column |
| Dependencies | `mido` and `python-rtmidi` in the service | none new; `mido` is Subsample's | none new |
| Release when the panel holding a pad disconnects | the service sends note-off or CC 123 | the service sends `fire` with `on: false`, or the adapter does on link loss | as the middle column |

The recommendation of this document is the middle column as the standard shape, because it is the only one that works wherever the service runs and keeps `fire` inside the one codec; the declaration keeps the `midi` block so that a co-located service may take the left column as a latency optimisation if `subsample-substation` and `latency` find the extra hop matters. The right column is excluded until the lock model is reviewed.

## A shared adapter package or a per-app module

| | Shared package all three depend on | Per-app module following a documented shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is shared as code | connect loop, backoff, envelope codec, snapshot handling, declaration builder (about 150 lines, judged from `supervisor/core.py:23-257`, which touches no host object) | nothing; the protocol specification and its conformance vectors are shared instead |
| What is per-app in both columns | the app half: which events, which locks, which loop, what a `set` or `fire` does; Supervisor's private-state reach (`supervisor/app/subsample.py:154-166`) lived here, not in the shared class | the same, in the same file as the link |
| An envelope change | one release of the package, then a dependency bump and reinstall in three repositories | three edits, each checked by the same vector table |
| A change to one app's half | one edit in that app, unchanged | the same |
| Drift | code cannot drift; the vectors still belong in the package's tests | three copies can drift; the vectors are what catch it, as #397 concluded for device matching after a prose contract drifted within hours |
| Deployment | a fourth package installable on every host; C-ws already adds `websockets` to every host, so this is one more dependency rather than a new kind of cost | nothing beyond the app and `websockets` |
| Coordination | #611 makes a coordinated release across the stack cheap, so a fourth repository is a small cost | none needed |

The two columns are close, and the difference is when to extract rather than whether. Recommended for v1: per-app modules plus a protocol specification with executable test vectors, published in the Superintendent project and run by each app's test suite in the manner #397 proposed for device matching. The grounds: the app half dominates each module and is per-app either way; the shareable part is small enough that three copies pinned by one vector table are cheaper than a fourth repository until the three modules exist; and extraction into a package once they have converged is a mechanical move that #611 makes easy. Whether Simon would rather start with the package is put to him separately.

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C-ws per-app module.** One adapter module in each package, own daemon thread and loop, one outbound WebSocket to the service, `manifest` and `snapshot` on connect, the frame types of #1916 and #1920 on the wire; inbound `set` and `fire` queued and applied at `reschedule_pulse` and `beat` in Subsequence, under the lock order or via MIDI in Subsample, by `call_soon_threadsafe` in Substation | one codec in the service for apps and browsers; keepalive, framing and close from the library; no new listening sockets; measured clock cost nil with the drain; Supervisor's threaded shape and mocks carry over as shapes (#1923) | `websockets` becomes an optional extra of Subsample and Substation and a floor bump to 13.0 in Subsequence; a connect loop and a codec appear in three files until they are pulled into a package |
| **C-tcp per-app module.** The same module over a stdlib TCP socket with newline-delimited JSON | zero new dependencies anywhere, including on a Pi; simplest possible client | the service grows a second framing and a hand-rolled keepalive; every envelope detail is written twice; no measured advantage on the clock |

A is excluded from the short list because it cannot carry a connect-time snapshot, cannot see an absent peer, does not fit a typed declaration, and reusing Subsequence's receiver would put Superintendent behind the user's OSC door on the LAN; it stays as the user-facing control surface it is. D is a complement carried by whichever option is chosen, not an alternative to it.

## Recommendation

C-ws as a per-app module, with the app-side rule per app fixed as part of the shape:

- Subsequence: the module runs the link on a daemon thread; inbound `set` frames go into a bounded `queue.Queue` (drop and count when full, never block); a `reschedule_pulse` listener drains it immediately before the rebuild that consumes the grid (`sequencer.py:1804`) and applies each write through the `StepGrid` helper from the loop, and a `beat` listener drains whatever arrived for patterns not rescheduling this pulse; verbs that must not wait a beat (`trigger()`, and mute or tempo if the panel wants them immediate) go through the existing `run_coroutine_threadsafe` and `call_soon_threadsafe` paths at the measured cost of one wake-up each. Outbound `changed` frames are built in a `pattern_reschedule` listener on the loop (cheap: the pattern's `steps` is already in hand) and handed to the link thread by reference swap; the legacy web UI's 100 ms throttle and unchanged-frame skip (`web_ui.py:175-196`) are the precedent for anything periodic. Nothing runs in `_process_pulse`.
- Subsample: the module runs the same link on its thread; it subscribes to `app_events` and `player.events` and copies under its own lock (Supervisor's shape); a `fire` on a declared pad is turned into note-on and note-off messages sent through a mido output the module opens to the process's own virtual port, which keeps the player's single-MIDI-thread invariant intact (the alternative where the co-located service sends the MIDI itself is kept in the pad-path table above); anything read from the player goes through `_snapshot_cc_state` or the named locks in the documented order.
- Substation: the module runs the same link on its thread; it registers sync handlers for `channel_state`, `recording_started`, `recording_saved` and `recording_discarded`, all of which land on the scanner loop by one route or the other, and for `noise_floor` and `channel_snr`, which run on the executor thread and where it swaps references rather than locking; it declares what exists today (band, channels, recordings) and, once Substation grows a band-switch verb (`subsample-substation`), forwards it with `call_soon_threadsafe`.

The reasons: it is the only option that gives the service one codec, one connection direction and one failure story for all three apps while adding no listening socket to any of them; its clock cost is nil when the app-side rule is followed, which was measured for the recommended shape itself, and the measurement shows the rule matters more than the transport; and it is the shape #1919 assumed when it recommended registration. The selector finding is recorded for `subsequence-grid` and `serving`: if the sequencer ever needs per-message immediacy on the loop, a `SelectSelector` loop or a wider spin margin removes the millisecond, and that is an engine change under #611, not a transport choice.

## Sketch of the documented adapter shape

One module per app, about this surface, prototypes exempt from house style but the modules not:

- `Adapter(url, manifest, apply, snapshot)`: `manifest()` returns the app's control tree (#1916); `apply(frame)` is the app-side rule above for `set` and `fire`; `snapshot()` returns the current values. `start()` spawns the daemon thread; `stop()` closes the link and joins with a timeout, as Supervisor's `stop_threaded()` does (`core.py:120-128`).
- The frames are #1916's, with no second vocabulary. On connect the app sends `manifest` (which already carries `app`, `contract`, `app_version` and `instance`, so it is the app's hello) and then `snapshot`. Inbound: `set {seq, path, v}`, `fire {seq, path, v}`, `ping {at}`. Outbound: `ack {seq, path, v}` and `nack {seq, path, reason}` answering a `set` or a failed `fire`, `changed {path, v, by}` when a value lands, `telemetry {path, v, at}` for meters, `event {path, name, data, at}`, `path_added` and `path_removed` on a live reload or `unregister()`, `pong {at}`. The one difference from the browser side is that the app's frames carry no `ver`: #1920 makes the service the owner of the per-app state version, so the service stamps `ver` and `app` on what it relays. Backoff on connect failure capped at a few seconds; log once at WARNING when the service is first absent, at INFO when it returns.
- Failure containment: every inbound frame is validated before it is queued; a bad frame is dropped and counted, never raised into the host; the inbound queue is bounded and drops when full; the outbound side is a latest-value dictionary swapped by reference plus Supervisor's dirty flag on the link loop, so a slow or dead service never accumulates work on the clock loop and never blocks the host. (The bench hands each beat frame to the link loop with `run_coroutine_threadsafe`, which is the measurement stand-in and not the sketch.)
- Conformance: a table of frames (`manifest`, `snapshot`, `set`, `fire`, `ack`, `nack`, `changed`, `event`, malformed cases) in the Superintendent project, executed by each app's tests against its module.

## Not covered

- Whether calling `_safe_handle_message` from an adapter thread is safe under Subsample's lock model was not tested; the recommendation avoids it by using the virtual port. `subsample-substation` should confirm the mido output path's latency on the real host, and that a process can open a mido output to its own virtual input port on ALSA (expected, not tried here).
- The WebSocket link measurements on the clock loop had a 2.74 ms outlier in one run; single runs, workstation, not repeated.
- The C-tcp drain was not run; its figure is inferred from the C-ws drain, whose app-side half is the same code.
- Which of the two pad paths is used when the service is co-located is `subsample-substation`'s to decide with `latency`'s numbers; this document fixes only that the adapter can carry `fire` everywhere.
