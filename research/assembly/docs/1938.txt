#1938  Substation switches band today only by restarting, but RadioScanner already stops cleanly on a queued sentinel, so a fresh scanner in the same process is a switch of about 40 ms plus the 2 s warm-up on the workstation; a process restart adds about 0.75 s of imports and, on an RTL-SDR with calibration on, about 13.7 s; the panel can show channel states, SNR meters and recordings today, and a recording waveform is a 1 ms envelope the adapter computes from the final WAV
  finding · active · #research · #subsample-substation · superintendent

Summary: Substation's band is fixed at RadioScanner construction and selected only by --band; there is no inbound control path, the OSC sender is not wired and the Supervisor hook is read-only. The scanner nevertheless has a clean stop that nothing external calls yet: a `None` sentinel on the sample queue ends `scan()` through its `finally`, which cancels the device read and closes every active recording with its `recording_saved` or `recording_discarded` event. Three routes to a new band follow. A process restart (today's route) pays interpreter and imports (measured 0.73 to 0.77 s for the whole package on the workstation), device open, RTL-SDR calibration when enabled (13 reads of 262144 samples at 300 kHz plus 2.3 s of sleeps, about 13.7 s computed from the code; only `RtlSdrDevice` has both `read_samples` and `freq_correction`, which the gate requires) and the ten-slice noise-floor warm-up (2 s on a live device). A fresh `RadioScanner` constructed in the same process after the sentinel, which a dozen lines around the CLI's single construction would give, pays the same device reopen, calibration and warm-up but neither the imports nor the link; executed here with the package's own `FileDevice` on a synthesised IQ file, the switch took 37 to 41 ms from request to the new band's first processed slice and about 150 ms to its warm-up complete, the latter processing-bound because a file has no real-time pacing. A retune inside a running `RadioScanner` would save the device reopen and the calibration but must close recordings, cancel a blocking read whose block size is fixed at start, drain the queued IQ, retune and re-rate the device, and rebuild about thirty band-derived attributes between two executor slices; whether the device accepts a retune while streaming is stated nowhere for HackRF or SoapySDR and only by rtl_fm's practice for RTL-SDR. Today the panel can show the static band, per-channel SNR at 5 Hz, channel state with tone, noise floor and recordings from events, with history only by scanning the audio directory. No waveform data exists for recordings; a 400-bin envelope of the final 16 kHz WAV costs about 1 ms and belongs in the adapter's `recording_saved` handler.

**Question.** What would a runtime band switch need inside `RadioScanner`, what can the panel show of Substation today, and how would a recording waveform be produced?

## The band is fixed at construction and nothing listens

`RadioScanner.__init__` takes `band_name` (`substation/scanner.py:71`) and derives everything from `self.config.bands[band_name]` (`scanner.py:88`, `99`): `freq_start`, `freq_end`, `channel_spacing`, `channel_width`, `sample_rate`, thresholds, `modulation`, `can_demod`, `can_record` (`scanner.py:115-160`); the channel list and its original indices (`scanner.py:170-193`); the centre frequency with its DC-avoidance shift and the required bandwidth (`scanner.py:205-221`); and per-channel dictionaries for last-active time, audio-silence time, state (`scanner.py:225`), SNR, tones, start times, warning times and recorders (`scanner.py:196-245`). `_setup_sdr` (`scanner.py:829`) applies `sample_rate` and `center_freq` to the device (`scanner.py:842`, `857`), adopting the device's actual values when they differ (`scanner.py:848-869`, which is how a file device's fixed centre wins), runs `_calibrate_sdr` when `calibration_frequency_hz` is set and the device has both `read_samples` and `freq_correction` (`scanner.py:894-898`, the gate at `897`), then `_precompute_fft_params()` (`scanner.py:901`, defined at `467`), which builds `samples_per_slice`, `fft_size`, the window, the frequency bins, per-channel bin ranges and masks (`scanner.py:486-613`), calls `_compute_noise_regions()` (`scanner.py:552`; the method is defined at `614`) for the noise regions, and sets up the channel filter and the per-channel oscillators. Runtime state that is band-shaped: `channel_filter_zi` (`scanner.py:275`), `channel_demod_state` (`278`), `sample_counter`, the noise-floor EMA and the warm-up counter (`scanner.py:289-290`, `NOISE_FLOOR_WARMUP_SLICES = 10` at `substation/constants.py:156`, the EMA alpha at `151`).

Only `RtlSdrDevice` passes the calibration gate: it has `read_samples` (`substation/devices/rtlsdr.py:132`) and `freq_correction` (`rtlsdr.py:73-92`). `SoapySdrDevice` has `read_samples`, written for calibration (`substation/devices/soapysdr.py:641`), but no `freq_correction`, so it is skipped by the second half of the condition; `HackRfDevice` and `FileDevice` have neither (`hackrf.py`, `file.py`, grep). The config docstring says the same (`substation/config.py:132-136`).

The CLI constructs one scanner per process from `--band` (`substation/cli.py:150-157`, `294`, `398`) and awaits `scan.scan()` once (`cli.py:163-167`); the README documents one process per device for parallel bands (`README.md:839-852`). There is no listening socket in the package; the OSC sender is not imported by the CLI (`substation/osc_sender.py:16-20`) and the Supervisor hook is a read-only broadcaster awaited on the scanner loop (`cli.py:157-167`, `240-252`). The six events on `RadioScanner.on()` (`scanner.py:362-439`; sync handlers with `loop=` are marshalled by `call_soon_threadsafe`, without `loop=` they run on the caller's thread, `scanner.py:402-439`) are emitted at `1438` (`recording_started`, with `loop=`), `1499` and `1515` (`recording_discarded`, from the stop coroutine), `1521` (`recording_saved`, after `ChannelRecorder.close()` has written headers and metadata, `scanner.py:1458-1523`, `substation/recording.py:819-880`), `1617` (`noise_floor`, every slice, executor thread), `1892` (`channel_state`, with `loop=`, deliberately after demodulation so the tone rides on it, #1478) and `1899` (`channel_snr`, every slice, executor thread, one record per channel in frequency order).

## The scanner already stops cleanly

`scan()` (`scanner.py:1922`) creates the sample queue, starts `sdr.read_samples_async(self._sdr_callback, self.samples_per_slice)` in an executor (`scanner.py:1953-1960`) and consumes slices with `run_in_executor(None, self._process_samples, samples, loop)` in an `async for` over `_sample_band_async()` (`scanner.py:1999-2006`). `_sample_band_async` (`scanner.py:1008`) returns when it takes a `None` from the queue (`scanner.py:1027-1028`), which ends the `async for`; `scan()`'s `finally` (`scanner.py:2016-2033`) then calls `sdr.cancel_read_async()` (`2020`) and awaits `asyncio.shield(self._cleanup_sdr())` (`2031`), and `_cleanup_sdr` (`scanner.py:903-912`) pops every active `ChannelRecorder` and awaits `_stop_channel_recording` for it, so each open recording is finalised and emits `recording_saved` or `recording_discarded`, before closing the device. The sentinel is queued by `_signal_stream_end` (`scanner.py:941-969`), which runs on the loop thread and, unlike a sample block, is never dropped: if the queue is full it discards one pending block to make room (`scanner.py:958-969`). Today it is called from three places, all inside the class: the device callback when a non-blocking backend signals end of stream (`scanner.py:971-990`), the streaming task's error path and its file-playback end-of-file path (`scanner.py:1953-1981`), and the task's done-callback (`scanner.py:1986-1993`). Nothing outside the class calls it, and it is private; a public `request_stop()` wrapping it is one line.

## Three routes to a new band

**A fresh `RadioScanner` in the same process.** `run_scanner` (`cli.py:123-171`) constructs one scanner and awaits `scan()` once; a loop around `cli.py:150-167` with a band variable, a request handed in from another thread by `loop.call_soon_threadsafe` (the Substation rule of #1928), and a call to the stop above is the whole change, about a dozen lines. Nothing is mutated in place: the new instance derives its band block, FFT parameters and per-channel dictionaries in its own constructor and `_setup_sdr`, and the old instance's `finally` has already closed its recordings and its device. What the route does not save: `_setup_sdr` on the new instance reopens the device and runs `_calibrate_sdr` again whenever `calibration_frequency_hz` is set (`scanner.py:894-898`), so calibration is paid per switch unless a small change carries the measured ppm from the old device into the new instance in place of the calibration; the noise-floor warm-up is paid again, correctly, since an EMA from another band is wrong. Two details for whoever writes it: handlers registered with `on()` belong to the instance, so the adapter (and the Supervisor hook, `cli.py:157-161`, while it exists) must re-register on each new scanner; and the process's `--band` argument no longer describes the running band after a switch, so a crash restart by systemd returns to the argv band unless the last requested band is persisted.

**A process restart.** Whatever runs Substation (a shell today, a systemd unit if it gains one) starts it again with a different `--band`. It performs every step the fresh instance performs plus interpreter start and imports, and it drops the adapter link, so the panel sees `app {present: false}` (#1920) until the new process registers.

**A retune inside the running instance.** The stream: the block size is baked into the read at start, and blocking backends "only return at end-of-stream, cancellation, or error" (`substation/devices/base.py:141-160`), so a band whose `sample_rate` differs (the defaults range from 1.024 MHz for `pmr` to 12.5 MHz for the air bands, `substation/config.yaml.default:214-232`) changes `samples_per_slice` and requires `cancel_read_async()` and a new streaming task; the queue may hold up to `sample_queue_maxsize` 200 slices of 200 ms, 40 s of old-band IQ (`config.yaml.default:1-16`), which must be drained before the new parameters are trusted. Every `_process_samples` call reads `self.*` on the executor thread, so the swap has to happen on the loop between two `await`s of the `async for` body (`scanner.py:1999-2010`), never while a slice is in flight. Recordings: every active `ChannelRecorder` must be closed through `_stop_channel_recording` so its file is finalised and its event fires (`scanner.py:903-912` is the sequence `_cleanup_sdr` runs); a recorder's flush task is a future on the loop (`scanner.py:1429-1433`). The device: whether a device accepts `center_freq` and `sample_rate` while streaming is not stated by any of the three bindings: pyrtlsdr documents `center_freq` as "Get/Set the center frequency of the device (in Hz)" and says nothing about concurrency (https://pyrtlsdr.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rtlsdr.html); `rtl-sdr.h` has no comment on `rtlsdr_set_center_freq` and none on calling setters during `rtlsdr_read_async` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/osmocom/rtl-sdr/master/include/rtl-sdr.h), though `rtl_fm`'s controller thread calls `rtlsdr_set_center_freq` while its dongle thread sits in `rtlsdr_read_async`, which is how it hops frequencies (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/osmocom/rtl-sdr/master/src/rtl_fm.c); `hackrf.h` documents `hackrf_set_freq` only as "Set the center frequency" (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/greatscottgadgets/hackrf/master/host/libhackrf/src/hackrf.h); SoapySDR documents `setFrequency` without a threading statement and says a stream "may not be used concurrently from multiple threads" (https://pothosware.github.io/SoapySDR/doxygen/latest/classSoapySDR_1_1Device.html). The device wrappers set frequency directly (`rtlsdr.py:58-60`, `hackrf.py:217-219`, `soapysdr.py:234-237`). The rebuild: re-derive the constructor's band block (`scanner.py:99-245`) and re-run `_precompute_fft_params()`; reset `_noise_floor_ema`, `_warmup_remaining`, `sample_counter`, `channel_filter_zi`, `channel_demod_state` and the per-channel dictionaries; keep `freq_correction`, which is a device property and not a band property. The `band` field in every event and in file paths (`recording.py:377`, `467`) follows `self.band_name`, so the adapter's manifest must be re-sent whichever route is taken. What this route saves over the fresh instance is the device reopen and the calibration; what it costs is new code across the executor boundary of a 2000-line class whose per-channel state lives in twelve dictionaries, and a device behaviour that is unverified for two of three bindings.

## Cost of a switch, by route

| Step | Process restart (today) | Fresh `RadioScanner` in the same process (a loop in `run_scanner`) | Retune in place (new code) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Close active recordings and emit their events | yes, `_cleanup_sdr` (`scanner.py:903-912`) | yes, the same `finally` | yes, the same coroutine called explicitly |
| Cancel the device read and drop queued IQ | yes, process exit | yes, `cancel_read_async()` in the `finally`; the queued backlog ahead of the sentinel is processed first, measured at 9.4 to 9.9 ms per queued slice here | yes; `cancel_read_async()` plus draining up to 40 s of queued slices |
| Interpreter and imports | 0.73 to 0.77 s wall for `import substation.cli` on the workstation (three runs; the numpy, scipy.signal, pydantic, soundfile and yaml floor alone 0.63 to 0.69 s; a bare interpreter under 10 ms), more on a Pi | none | none |
| Construct the scanner | yes | 0.2 to 0.4 ms measured | none |
| Open the device, set rate and frequency | yes | yes (close in the old `finally`, open in the new `_setup_sdr`); 16 to 24 ms with the file device here, USB cost on a real device unmeasured | set rate and frequency only |
| RTL-SDR calibration when `calibration_frequency_hz` is set (default 93.7 MHz, `config.yaml.default:19`) | 3 warm-up reads with 0.1 s sleeps (`scanner.py:726-731`) plus 10 measured reads with 0.2 s settling sleeps (`scanner.py:741-773`), each read 262144 samples at 300 kHz: 11.4 s of reads plus 2.3 s of sleeps plus ten 262144-point FFTs, about 13.7 s by the code; skipped for HackRF, SoapySDR and file devices, which fail the `read_samples` and `freq_correction` gate | the same, since `_setup_sdr` runs it again; none if a change carries the ppm forward | none; `freq_correction` survives |
| `_precompute_fft_params` and the band block | yes | yes, inside the new `_setup_sdr` | yes |
| Noise-floor warm-up before detection | 10 slices of 200 ms, 2 s | the same 2 s on a live device (10 slices at full speed took 128 to 144 ms with the file device) | the same 2 s unless the EMA is deliberately kept, which is wrong across bands |
| Adapter link | drops and reconnects; `app {present: false}` meanwhile; manifest and snapshot re-sent | stays up; handlers re-registered on the new instance; `manifest` and `snapshot` re-sent | stays up; `manifest` re-sent |
| Code | none | about a dozen lines in `cli.py` and one public stop method; a `RadioScanner` constructor argument if the ppm is to be carried | a new verb on a 2000-line class whose per-channel state lives in twelve dictionaries read from an executor thread |
| Measured here (workstation, file device, no calibration) | not run; the scanner needs a device or an IQ file, and the figure is the sum of the rows above: about 0.75 s plus device open plus 2 s | 37 to 41 ms from the stop request to the new band's first processed slice with a two-slice backlog (190 to 195 ms with an eighteen-slice backlog); about 150 ms to the new band's warm-up complete, processing-bound | not run |

So on a live RTL-SDR with calibration enabled every route but the in-place retune pays about 13.7 s per switch, on top of the 2 s warm-up all three pay; with calibration disabled, or a non-RTL device, the fresh instance costs the switch about 2 s plus a device reopen, the restart about 0.75 s more plus a dropped link, and the retune about 2 s with nothing else. Restart wall-clock was not measured because the working tree has no device and the scanner cannot run without one; the file-device figures below are the part that could be measured without hardware.

## Measurements

`restart_file.py` under the scratchpad `subsample-substation/restart/` (a prototype, not house style) synthesises an 8 s two-channel PCM16 IQ WAV at 1.024 MHz (Gaussian noise at 0.01 RMS plus a signal on PMR channel 12 at 446.14375 MHz), defines two bands `probe_a` (16 channels) and `probe_b` (8 channels) over the same range in `restart_config.yaml` with `calibration_frequency_hz: null` and recordings directed under the scratchpad, and runs three `RadioScanner` instances in one process with `device_type='file'` (`substation/devices/__init__.py:104-111`; `FileDevice` at `substation/devices/file.py:77-149` ignores a differing sample rate and takes its centre from the caller, `file.py:135-149`; its reader blocks in the calling executor thread until end of file or `cancel_read_async()`, `file.py:204-268`). Each instance is stopped after a given number of processed slices by calling `_signal_stream_end()` on the loop, exactly where an adapter's `call_soon_threadsafe` would land, and the next instance is constructed after `scan()` returns. `_process_samples` is wrapped on the instance to timestamp the first slice and each slice's duration; `noise_floor` with `warmup_complete` marks the end of warm-up. Runs on 2026-09-03 on the development workstation (Intel i7-9700K, Python 3.13.11, load average 0.3 to 0.5), results in `results_restart_file.txt` (no clock, sentinel after 14 slices), `results_restart_file_vclock.txt` and `results_restart_file_fm_vclock.txt`, `results_restart_file_fm3_vclock.txt` (the package's `VirtualClock`, sentinel after 30 of 32 slices), each run three instances long:

- interpreter plus imports of numpy, `substation.scanner` and `substation.config` inside the script: 642 to 662 ms; `python -c "import substation.cli"` from the shell: 0.73 to 0.77 s wall (`results_import_time.txt`).
- `RadioScanner.__init__`: 0.2 to 0.4 ms.
- `scan()` called to the first `_process_samples` (file device open, `_setup_sdr`, `_precompute_fft_params`, first slice): 16.4 to 23.8 ms.
- `scan()` called to warm-up complete: 128 to 144 ms, ten slices at 9.4 to 9.9 ms each with no real-time pacing; on a live device this is 10 slices of 200 ms by construction.
- stop requested to `scan()` returned (backlog drained, read cancelled, `_cleanup_sdr`): 20.5 to 21.6 ms with two slices queued; 172 to 178 ms in the run where the file device had queued eighteen slices ahead of the sentinel, which is the backlog times the per-slice cost and stands in for the drain a live device pays only when processing is behind.
- stop requested to the next instance's first processed slice: 37.4 to 40.5 ms (two-slice backlog), 189.7 to 195.0 ms (eighteen-slice backlog); to the next instance's warm-up complete: 148.7 to 156.3 ms.
- `_process_samples` on the quiet 16-channel probe band: mean 9.4 to 9.9 ms per 200 ms slice, 19 ms when the per-channel phase ran.

No recording was open when the switches landed, so the close-recordings step of the `finally` was not exercised by the prototype: the synthesised signals never turned a channel on. A pure carrier gave a per-channel SNR of 0.6 dB because channel power is the mean of per-bin dB over the channel's 337 bins (`scanner.py:1307-1330`), an FM carrier at 2.5 kHz deviation gave 3.6 to 3.8 dB against the PMR threshold of 4.5 dB, and a three-tone FM carrier at 4 kHz deviation and 0.5 amplitude reached the per-channel phase (processing rose to 19 ms) but was rejected by Gate 1, the RF-level segment-power variance check that rejects a stationary signal (`scanner.py:1727-1758`), since the probe's carrier is on from the file's first sample. The path itself is read, not run: `scanner.py:903-912` and `1458-1523`.

## How a recording waveform would be produced

No waveform or peak data exists in Substation. On this machine the audio directory holds 7355 `.analysis.json` sidecars beside 7335 WAVs and 11 FLACs; 7346 sidecars are at `analysis_version` 14 and 9 at 13, and none has a `preview` block: Subsample's cataloguing tool wrote them with `with_preview=False` (`subsample/tools/catalog_samples.py:427`), so the Subsample bridge already runs over Substation's output here, but without envelopes.

| Where | Cost measured here | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inside `ChannelRecorder`, at `close()` | not measured; the audio has already been flushed to disk in blocks, so it means re-reading the file or accumulating min and max per flush | a change to `recording.py` and a new field on `recording_saved` |
| In the Substation adapter module, in its `recording_saved` handler | 400-bin min/max of the final WAV: 0.91 ms for 2 s and 1.04 ms for 64 s of 16 kHz audio with Subsample's routine, plus the file read; runs on the adapter's own thread after the event lands on the loop | `soundfile` (already a dependency) and one function; no scanner change; Subsample's int8 400-bin format so the panel has one waveform widget |
| In the service | the same arithmetic | the service must read the file: co-location or a shared filesystem, and a directory allowlist as Supervisor had (`supervisor/core.py:131-172`) |
| In the browser from the WAV | `decodeAudioData` 0.95 ms for 2 s and 8.7 ms for 64 s in headless Chromium 151, envelope 0.1 to 2 ms, fetch 1 to 6 ms on loopback | the audio file served to the browser (65 KB to 2 MB per view); FLAC in the audio element from Chrome 56 and Firefox 51 (https://caniuse.com/flac), not verified through `decodeAudioData` |
| Through Subsample's `/sample/import` | full analysis 291 to 369 ms for 2 s and 4.5 to 4.7 s for 64 s after a 12 s first-call warm-up; produces the full preview block and makes the recording playable | Subsample running with the OSC receiver enabled; the existing bridge (`osc_sender.py` `/sample/import`) |

## Hardware-gated

- Device close and reopen cost, and whether each binding accepts `center_freq` and `sample_rate` while `read_samples_async` is running, on the real RTL-SDR, HackRF and AirSpy hardware.
- Switch and restart wall-clock on the headless server and on the Pi, with and without `calibration_frequency_hz`; the file-device figures here omit USB and the 2 s real-time warm-up.
- The adapter envelope's cost on a Pi (workstation figure 1 ms).

## Not covered

- The in-place retune was not prototyped; its rebuild list is from reading the class. The fresh-instance route was executed instead, and with that route recommended in the companion design the in-place list is what a future Substation wave would need, not what the panel needs.
- The close-recordings step of the stop was not exercised by the prototype, for the Gate 1 reason above; it is read at `scanner.py:903-912`.
- The `latency` point had not filed when this was written.
