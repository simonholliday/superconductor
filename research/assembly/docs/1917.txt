#1917  The iiyama T2252MSC-B2 is a 10-point PCAP USB HID panel, driverless on Windows and expected to bind to Linux hid-multitouch through its generic multitouch catch-alls; its controller identity and iiyama's own Linux statement are unverified
  finding · active · #research · #ui-host · superintendent

Summary: The T2252MSC-B2 is a 21.5-inch 1920x1080 IPS panel with 10-point projected-capacitive touch over USB HID, 225 cd/m² with touch, glossy anti-fingerprint 7H glass (no anti-glare), HDMI and DisplayPort, a two-port USB 3.2 hub, tilt -5° to +90°, VESA 100. Windows 10 and 11 need no driver because Windows-8-class HID digitizers report multi-touch natively; the Linux in-tree driver hid-multitouch ends its device table with two catch-alls (generic multitouch and generic Windows-8 multitouch) that match any vendor and product, so a HID multitouch digitizer is expected to get multi-touch on a stock kernel, Raspberry Pi OS included, without a vendor driver. That expectation is not yet a fact for this panel: the controller identity is unpublished, iiyama's own OS-support PDFs are CAPTCHA-gated from this workstation, and the only sibling evidence is a vendor table listing Linux as mouse-only. Nothing in the three apps or Supervisor handles touch. This workstation is headless with no browser, so a probe page was written as the instrument for the panel host; the controller ID, standby behaviour, flat mounting and glare are hardware-gated. This is the panel finding for the `ui-host` point of #1912.

**Question.** What does the iiyama ProLite T2252MSC-B2 deliver as a touch panel, and what does the host need to drive it?

## Touch on the host: what the standards say

The panel presents as a USB HID touch digitizer (the datasheet's "HID" qualifier). Windows has required multi-touch digitizers to expose a HID touch-screen top-level collection with Contact Identifier and Contact Count Maximum since Windows 8 (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/design/component-guidelines/supporting-usages-in-multitouch-digitizer-drivers-win8), so on Windows 10 and 11 no vendor driver is needed for 10-contact reporting.

On Linux the in-tree driver `hid-multitouch` ("HID multitouch panels") ends its device table with two catch-alls that match any vendor and any product: `/* Generic MT device */ { HID_DEVICE(HID_BUS_ANY, HID_GROUP_MULTITOUCH, HID_ANY_ID, HID_ANY_ID) }` (lines 2721 to 2722 of the copy of https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/drivers/hid/hid-multitouch.c downloaded 2026-09-02) and `/* Generic Win 8 certified MT device */ { .driver_data = MT_CLS_WIN_8, HID_DEVICE(HID_BUS_ANY, HID_GROUP_MULTITOUCH_WIN_8, HID_ANY_ID, HID_ANY_ID) }` (lines 2724 to 2727). Before them sit explicit eGalax/EETI entries under `USB_VENDOR_ID_DWAV` (lines 2331 to 2397, the last a Windows-8-group entry for the C002 controller with class `MT_CLS_EGALAX_P80H84`). The group is assigned from the report descriptor, not from a vendor table, so a HID multitouch digitizer binds to hid-multitouch whether or not it carries the Windows 8 certification; the expectation of multi-touch on a stock kernel, Raspberry Pi OS included, rests on the panel being a HID multitouch digitizer, which the datasheet states, and not on the unverified Windows-8 certification.

What iiyama itself claims for Linux is not confirmed here: the "driver instruction" and "touch drivers and supported operating systems" PDFs (https://iiyama.com/f/c87911c87d4c6cebd419a12133163b29_driver-instruction-eeti-02-2021.pdf and https://iiyama.com/f/556fb7cb911577a64334d2b676ddf31e_texx03-touch-drivers-supported-operating-systems.pdf) are Cloudflare-challenge pages from this workstation. For a sibling model (T2735MSC-B2) a forum reader of iiyama's documentation reported Linux support listed as "Default mouse (click, drag, double-click and right-click)" only, with the vendor driver x86-only (https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=243524), while that model enumerated on a Raspberry Pi as `ID 0543:2e53 ViewSonic Corp.` (https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=248204). Vendor claims for Linux are conservative; the kernel's generic class decides in practice. Which controller the T2252MSC-B2 carries (EETI, ILITEK, ViewSonic or other), and whether its descriptor lands it in the multitouch group, is not published on any source read and is the first hardware check.

## Measurement environment and the probe page

This workstation (hpz2g4, Ubuntu kernel 6.8.0-138, i7-9700K) is headless (`XDG_SESSION_TYPE=tty`), has no browser installed and no input devices beyond USB root hubs, so nothing about touch delivery or rendering can be measured on it. The instrument for the panel host is `/tmp/claude-1000/-home-si/243c6ca9-504c-48de-a06d-eefc5c35da94/scratchpad/ui-host/touch-probe.html` (a prototype, not product code). Opened full-screen on the panel host it logs `navigator.maxTouchPoints`, every `pointerdown`, `pointerup` and `pointercancel` with `pointerId`, `pointerType`, `isPrimary`, the peak number of concurrent contacts, whether `contextmenu` fires on long-press, whether pinch leaks past `touch-action: none` (via `visualViewport.scale`), whether text selection leaks, and the requestAnimationFrame frame time while repainting a 1024-cell canvas. It applies the full page-side suppression set (`touch-action: none`, `overscroll-behavior: none`, `user-select: none`, `cursor: none`, `contextmenu` preventDefault) so a leak it reports is a host leak.

## Hardware-gated

- `lsusb` vendor:product of the touch controller and `dmesg` showing which driver binds (`hid-multitouch` versus `usbhid` mouse emulation); `libinput list-devices` capability line and `evtest` slot count (expect 10).
- Whether the panel still reports touch while the display is in standby and whether a touch wakes the host.
- The physical meaning of "90° up": whether the stand lets the glass lie face-up flat, or VESA mounting is needed for a flat surface.
- Reflection of studio luminaires at the intended angle; whether an anti-glare film is wanted on the glossy -B2.
- Palm rejection quality with a resting hand during two-finger gestures.
- The panel's touch report rate (from `evtest` timestamps), for the `latency` point.
