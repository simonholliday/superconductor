Rendering measurements at 128 and 1024 cells show that incremental updates fit inside a frame in every technique on a desktop and at a Raspberry Pi 5-class throttle, that a whole-grid change is a one-frame hitch there and a dropped-frame run at Pi 4 class, and that cost does not decide between DOM and canvas for the actual workload; DOM cells with a transform-moved playhead overlay plus canvas layers for continuously redrawn content is recommended because it gives touch hit-testing, labels and per-contact pointer events for nothing and depends least on the host's GPU path, while WebGL is excluded because it is the fastest technique with acceleration and the slowest without it and the Pi's Chromium cannot be relied on to have it. Preact plus htm vendored as one standalone module is recommended as the framework on the strength of being the smallest buildless option whose idioms the two prior codebases already use, with Lit the equal alternative that maps a control type onto a custom element; Svelte and Vue are excluded for needing a build or offering nothing extra. Every licence was checked at its primary source and every candidate is AGPL-3.0-compatible, the two GPL-3.0 widget sources only as separate modules. No existing widget library carries the two-contact gestures or matches the glass look and Subsample's 400-bin envelope is already the right waveform input, so the widgets should be written and the hardware conventions borrowed: hue for state, luminance for velocity, a distinct moving playhead colour, steady versus blinking for edit versus playing page, tempo-synced pulsing, a global brightness control, a small palette, square cells of one size in a fixed grid that pages rather than shrinks, controls outside the grid in a different shape from cells, 3:1 state contrast, flashing slower than three per second, and a 32-step maximum per grid view derived from the panel's 0.249 mm pixel pitch and WCAG target sizes.

**Question.** How is the Superintendent surface drawn, and with what libraries, given a browser-only host that may be a Raspberry Pi, no internet at run time, and AGPL-3.0 for everything shipped?

## Rendering technique for the grid and playhead

Measured on this workstation in headless Chromium 151, both through SwiftShader (software compositing and raster, standing for a host whose Chromium has lost acceleration) and through its GeForce GTX 1660, unthrottled and at CPU throttles that Speedometer places at a Raspberry Pi 5-class main thread (6x) and a Pi 4-class one (16x); the numbers, method and calibration are in the companion finding. The shape of the result: incremental updates (a playhead move and a handful of toggles) cost under 1 ms per frame at 1024 cells in every technique at the Pi 5-class throttle and 0.3 to 2 ms at the Pi 4-class one, so the first use-case's 16x8 grid is noise and a 1024-cell page is comfortable; a genuine change to every cell of a 1024-cell grid costs 8.6 ms in the DOM and 10.9 ms in SVG at Pi 5 class (one dropped frame) and 23 to 29 ms at Pi 4 class (every frame dropped), against 3.9 ms of JavaScript for a whole-canvas repaint that is nevertheless raster-bound without a GPU (66 of 300 frames dropped at 6x, none with the GPU); moving the playhead as one `transform`-driven overlay costs 30 to 45 percent less than toggling classes on two columns; a Preact re-render of the whole grid costs six to seven times the imperative incremental path; WebGL is the cheapest technique with a GPU and the only one that dropped frames at every throttle without one; and a 400-bin waveform polygon costs ten times more as an SVG `points` rewrite than as a canvas fill.

| Technique | Cost at 1024 cells, Pi 5-class throttle (finding) | Playhead | Text labels, hit-testing, focus | Pi risk | Fit for faders, meters, waveform | Licence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DOM cells (CSS grid of elements, class toggles, custom properties) with a canvas layer where content is redrawn continuously | Incremental 0.5 to 0.7 ms with style and layout; every-cell change 8.6 ms, one dropped frame per snapshot; 23 ms at Pi 4 class | One absolutely positioned column moved with `transform`: 0.3 to 0.5 ms per frame against 0.5 to 0.7 for class toggles, and the move itself costs no style or layout (measured; https://web.dev/articles/stick-to-compositor-only-properties-and-manage-layer-count) | Free: pointer events per cell, `pointerId` per contact, ARIA, CSS focus states, real text | Lowest: whole-grid changes are main-thread-bound, so they degrade with CPU speed rather than with the GPU path; needs neither GPU raster nor WebGL | Faders and selectors are DOM; meters and waveform are canvas, redrawn only when data changes | Browser API, none |
| Canvas 2D for everything (one canvas per widget or layered canvases per MDN) | Dirty-cell 0.1 to 0.3 ms at every throttle, the cheapest incremental path measured; whole repaint 3.9 ms of JavaScript but 66 of 300 frames dropped without a GPU (none with one) | Redraw the two affected columns | All by hand: hit-testing, labels (MDN warns text is costly), no focus or ARIA | Low for dirty-cell drawing; a full-screen repaint depends on software raster speed when acceleration is absent | Natural for meters and waveform; faders and selectors need custom drawing and input code | Browser API, none |

Excluded from the short list: WebGL. With the GPU it is the cheapest technique by far (negligible main-thread cost and no dropped frames at any throttle); without it, under SwiftShader, it dropped frames at every throttle level on the realistic load and was the slowest technique by an order of magnitude in the earlier harness. It also buys nothing the grid needs, adds a shader toolchain and hand-built hit-testing, and depends on Chromium's GPU path being healthy on the Pi, which a July 2025 forum report shows is not guaranteed (Chromium at 10 fps on a WebGL test while Firefox ran 60 fps on the same Pi 5, until a stale profile was removed; enabling Vulkan then disabled WebGL) (https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=390106). WebGL 2 is otherwise universal in current browsers (Chrome 56, Firefox 51, Safari 15, Edge 79; https://caniuse.com/webgl2), and OffscreenCanvas nearly so (Chrome 69, Firefox 105, Safari 17, Edge 79; https://caniuse.com/offscreencanvas), so the door stays open for a later spectrogram or scope.

**Recommendation: DOM cells with a `transform`-moved playhead overlay, plus canvas layers for continuously redrawn content (waveform, meters).** Cost does not decide between DOM and canvas for the actual workload: a column moves per step and a handful of cells change per beat, both are well under a millisecond per frame at Pi 5 class, and dirty-cell canvas is nominally cheaper. What decides it is that the DOM gives touch hit-testing, labels, per-contact pointer events, focus and ARIA for nothing, and that its worst case (a whole-grid change) is bound to main-thread speed, which the throttle calibrates, rather than to the host's raster path, which nothing here can. The mixed-page measurement used exactly this shape (512 DOM cells, eight `transform`-driven faders and one canvas waveform: about 0.5 ms per frame on the desktop, 1.2 ms at Pi 5 class, never a dropped frame). In software-rendered Firefox the DOM grid held 60 fps on the realistic load and dropped to 47 fps only with every cell changing every frame, so the recommendation holds there for the workload that matters. Rules that follow from the measurements: never re-render the grid through the virtual DOM per frame (measured at six to seven times the imperative path); move the playhead with `transform` only (measured); redraw canvases only on data change, not on `requestAnimationFrame`; apply a snapshot or page switch as one batch and accept a one-frame hitch on Pi 5-class hosts, and on Pi 4-class hosts with pages over 512 cells either stage the change over two or three frames or keep pages smaller; keep glow effects (`box-shadow`, `filter`) off cells that change per step.

## A visual language for music hardware on glass

What the hardware does, from the vendors' own documents:

- Ableton Push 3: the pads form an "8x8 pad grid". In the drum step sequencer "The 16 Drum Rack pads are laid out on the bottom left side of the pad grid, in a 4x4 arrangement", the rest of the grid is the step area, and at the default sixteenth-note resolution "two pages of steps are available at a time, for a total of two bars". Step colours: "Gray — this step doesn't contain a note. The clip's color — this step contains a note. Higher velocities are indicated by brighter pads. Lighter version of the clip's color — this step contains a note, but the note is muted. Unlit — the right two columns of pads will be unlit if triplets are selected as the step size." "During playback, the currently playing step is indicated by the moving green pad in the step sequencer. When Record is enabled, the moving pad will be red." (https://www.ableton.com/en/push/manual/). Drum Rack pads: track colour has a sound, gray empty, green playing, white selected, dark blue soloed, darker track colour muted.
- Ableton Push 2 interface: a 128-entry RGB palette; LED animations are one-shot, pulsing or blinking with durations from 24th notes to half notes, phase advanced by MIDI clock (`0xF8`, 1/24 beat) and reset by MIDI start/continue; brightness is a global multiplier (https://github.com/Ableton/push-interface/blob/master/doc/AbletonPush2MIDIDisplayInterface.asc).
- Novation Launchpad X: a 128-entry palette addressed by velocity (page 12); MIDI channel 1 static, 2 flashing, 3 pulsing; flashing alternates two colours at 50 percent duty over one beat, pulsing runs dark to full over two beats, both synchronised to MIDI clock or 120 BPM when none (page 13); a global brightness setting 0 to 127 exposed as eight levels (page 25) "to something comfortable for the environment the device is used in" (page 5); the lighting surface is "the 8x8 grid" plus "the top row of buttons, the logo and the right-side buttons" (page 9) (https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/s3fs-public/downloads/Launchpad%20X%20-%20Programmers%20Reference%20Manual.pdf).
- Elektron Digitakt: "Place note trigs on the sequencer using the sixteen [TRIG] keys." "Note trigs are indicated by red [TRIG] keys and lock trigs are indicated by yellow [TRIG] keys. Unlit [TRIG] keys indicate steps that does not contain any trigs." "If the pattern contains more than 16 steps, select the pattern page you want to edit by pressing the [PAGE] key. A fully lit <PATTERN PAGE> LED shows the active pattern page." While playing, the page currently being played is shown by a flashing PATTERN PAGE LED (https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1275776/Elektron-Digitakt.html?page=28, ?page=27). "The [TRIG] key of the locked trig blinks red (for note trigs) or yellow (for lock trigs) to indicate the trig now contains a parameter lock." (?page=33, section 10.11.1).
- Akai MPC X pad colour modes: Classic Velocity (red high, yellow low), Off/Dim/Bright to Velocity where brightness follows velocity (https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1282429/Akai-Mpcx.html?page=190).

What transfers to glass:

- Hue says what a cell is (empty, note, lock or accent, muted, selected, soloed); luminance says how much (velocity, probability). Every device above encodes velocity as brightness within the state's hue.
- The playhead is a distinct colour moving through the grid (green on Push), never a change to the cell's own hue. On glass, a translucent column overlay preserves the cell's state underneath, and it is also the cheapest way to move it (finding).
- Edit page versus playing page: steady for the page being edited, blinking for the page being played (Digitakt). This is the multi-page convention the first use-case needs.
- Tempo-synced animation: pulsing over two beats for "pending" or "armed", flashing over one beat for "attention"; both derived from the transport rather than wall-clock so they stay in step with the playhead.
- A single global brightness control, because the panel sits in changing studio light.
- A small palette. Both Push and Launchpad work from 128 entries; a surface palette of one identity colour per instrument plus a handful of state colours is enough and keeps contrast checkable.
- Shape: cells are squares of one size in a fixed grid, and content pages rather than the grid shrinking (Push shows two pages of 16 steps in the same pads; Digitakt shows one page of 16 on the same sixteen keys). Pads and steps are distinct blocks (Push's 4x4 drum bank beside the step area), so a pad bank and a step grid should not share a widget. Transport, page and mode controls sit outside the grid and look different from cells (Launchpad's top row and right-side buttons; Digitakt's [PAGE] key and PATTERN PAGE LEDs), so page indicators should be a row of small lights or tabs outside the grid, not a special row of cells. Cells that the current mode cannot use stay in place unlit (Push's triplet columns) rather than disappearing, so the grid never reflows.

Readability under studio lighting: keep at least 3:1 between a cell state and its neighbours and between states of the same cell (WCAG 1.4.11 applies to component states: "any visual information necessary to indicate state, such as whether a component is selected or focused must also ensure that the information used to identify the control in that state has a minimum 3:1 contrast ratio", https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html) and 4.5:1 for labels. Perceived contrast depends on the room: "Perception of color in a well-lit room will be different than perception of that same color on that same computer screen in a dark room", and `prefers-contrast` plus the Ambient Light Sensor exist for adapting (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Guides/Colors_and_Luminance). Sund (Medical Physics, 2022) found that low luminance settings lose fine detail in dark regions and "should be avoided" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9305559/). The T2252MSC-B2 is a 21.5-inch 1920x1080 IPS panel rated 250 cd/m² and "225 cd/m² with touch", pixel pitch 0.249 mm, projective capacitive touch with 10 points over USB HID, with an edge-to-edge glass front (retailer specification sheets: https://www.targetcomponents.co.uk/graphics-displays-and-software/displays/monitors/moiiy-t2252mscb2 and https://www.enscomputers.co.uk/product/iiyama-prolite-t2252msc-b2-21-5-inch-10-point-touchscreen-ips-monitor-full-hd-5ms-usb-hub-hdmi-displayport-speakers-black-internal-psu-vesa/; the 21.5-inch, 1920x1080, 10-point PCAP and edge-to-edge glass figures also at https://monitorlounge.com/products/iiyama-prolite-t2252msc-b2-21-5-p-cap-10pt-ips-touch-screen-and-edge-to-edge-glass), so fine hairlines on near-black are the thing to avoid; use filled shapes and a dark grey rather than pure black ground so reflections do not swallow the lowest states. Flashing must stay under three flashes per second (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Guides/Colors_and_Luminance): a one-beat flash at 180 BPM is exactly three per second, so the surface should flash at two beats or slower, or pulse.

Touch geometry on this panel: the 0.249 mm pixel pitch makes one CSS pixel about 0.25 mm at device pixel ratio 1. WCAG 2.2 sets a 24 CSS pixel floor for targets (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); Apple's guideline is 44 pt and Material's 48 dp. Sixteen columns across the full width gives 120 px (about 30 mm) cells; thirty-two gives 60 px (15 mm); sixty-four gives 30 px (7.5 mm), above the floor but under the platform recommendations. So the grid should show at most 32 steps of a pattern at once and page beyond that, which is also what the hardware does.

## Licence compatibility

From the FSF's list (https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.en.html): Apache 2.0 is "compatible with version 3 of the GNU GPL"; Expat/MIT, modified BSD and ISC are lax permissive licences compatible with the GPL; LGPL 2.1 and 3 are compatible with GPLv3; and for GPL-3.0 code, "you are allowed to combine separate modules or source files released under both of those licenses in a single project" with AGPLv3. The compatibility column in each table above follows from that. Rendering techniques (DOM, SVG, Canvas 2D, WebGL) are browser APIs and carry no licence. The GPL-3.0 sources (Open Stage Control, Faust UI) are usable as separate modules but not as code merged into AGPL files. The licence-audit point should carry the one consolidated table.
