projects/#1926  Every adapter that wakes Subsequence's clock loop per message costs about 1 ms at p99 whatever the transport, a queue drained at the sequencer's own hooks costs nothing, and the cause is asyncio's epoll millisecond rounding; Subsample admits only locked reads and MIDI in, Substation only sync handlers on the events its emitter delivers
  finding · active · #research · #transport-apps · superintendent

Summary: Per app, the concurrency model fixes what an in-process adapter may do: in Subsequence one asyncio loop owns the clock and every MIDI send, asyncio locks are loop-only, and every sanctioned cross-thread entry marshals onto `_sequencer._event_loop`; in Subsample there is no loop, a written lock order, events on recorder, worker and rtmidi threads, and MIDI into the virtual port is the only trigger path; in Substation the scanner loop is not a clock, handlers reach the loop either by `loop=` marshalling from the executor thread or by being emitted from a coroutine already on it, the per-slice events run on the executor thread, and there is no inbound path at all. Measured with the real `Sequencer` by the `clock_jitter.py` method (16 bars at 120 BPM, 50 Hz inbound commands, one frame per beat out, development workstation): OSC on the loop, OSC on a thread with `call_soon_threadsafe` per datagram, a WebSocket link on the loop or on a thread, and a TCP link on a thread all cost p99 1.05 to 1.22 ms and 30 to 59 pulses over 1 ms in 1536, while the same OSC server or WebSocket link on a thread with its messages queued and drained at the `beat` event, or at `reschedule_pulse` with a real repeating pattern, costs p99 0.001 to 0.002 ms against a 0.002 ms baseline. The cause is CPython's `EpollSelector` rounding every timeout up to a whole millisecond, so a mid-sleep wake-up pushes the clock's sleep past its 1 ms spin margin; under a `SelectSelector` loop the same per-message traffic costs p99 0.001 to 0.018 ms. `reschedule_pulse` is awaited immediately before the rebuild that consumes the grid, so a drain there adds no musical latency.

**Question.** What does each music app's concurrency model allow an in-process Superintendent adapter to do, from which thread, and what does the adapter cost Subsequence's clock?

## Summary

All three apps already run something Superintendent-shaped beside their real-time work, and each has a documented rule for crossing into it. Subsequence's rule is "hand it to the loop" (`run_coroutine_threadsafe` or `call_soon_threadsafe` onto `_sequencer._event_loop`); Subsample's is "take the named lock, or subscribe and copy"; Substation's is "emit with `loop=` from off the loop, or emit from a coroutine that is already on it, and the scanner's `emit()` does the rest". None of the three has a place where an adapter may block, and only Subsequence has a clock that a wake-up can disturb. Measured on the real `Sequencer` with the `clock_jitter.py` method, every adapter shape that wakes the clock loop per message costs the same tail, about 1.1 ms at p99 and 1.5 ms at maximum at fifty messages a second, whether the socket is on the loop or on a thread; an adapter that queues on a thread and applies at the sequencer's own `reschedule_pulse` or `beat` hook costs nothing measurable, and that was measured for the WebSocket link the companion design recommends as well as for OSC. The tail is a property of the loop's sleep, not of the transport: asyncio's default epoll selector rounds every wait up to a whole millisecond, so a mid-sleep wake-up makes the clock's sleep overshoot its one-millisecond spin margin; with a `SelectSelector` the same traffic costs nothing.

## Subsequence: one asyncio loop owns the clock and every MIDI send

- `Composition.play()` is `asyncio.run(self._run())` (`subsequence/composition.py:5410-5421`); `_run()` starts the live REPL, the OSC server and the legacy web UI on that loop before `run_until_stopped` (`composition.py:5861-5883`). `Sequencer.start()` records the running loop in `self._event_loop` "for thread-safe scheduling (e.g., trigger() from user threads)" (`subsequence/sequencer.py:1289-1290`) and creates the clock task (`sequencer.py:1294`).
- The clock is `_run_loop_internal_clock` (`sequencer.py:1489-1572`): per pulse it fires bar and beat events, sends MIDI clock, awaits `_advance_pulse()`, then sleeps to within `_spin_threshold` (1 ms, `sequencer.py:465`) of the next pulse and spins for the rest (`sequencer.py:1553-1564`); the jitter log is appended after the spin (`sequencer.py:1566-1567`). Every callback that runs on this loop delays the pulse that follows it.
- Locks are asyncio locks (`queue_lock`, `pattern_lock`, `callback_lock`, `sequencer.py:428-433`), so they are usable only from the loop thread: "Almost all asyncio objects are not thread safe" (https://docs.python.org/3/library/asyncio-dev.html).
- Events reach observers through `EventEmitter.emit_async` (`subsequence/event_emitter.py:75-109`), which calls a sync listener inline and logs its exception without stopping the others (`event_emitter.py:95-99`). `bar` and `beat` are spawned as tasks from the pulse (`sequencer.py:1416`, `1427`; `_spawn` at `932-944`), so they run after that pulse's MIDI. `reschedule_pulse(pulse, patterns)` is awaited inline before any rebuild (`sequencer.py:1804`) and `pattern_reschedule(pattern, cycle_start_pulse)` is spawned after each (`sequencer.py:1829`). A failing rebuild is contained to that pattern's cycle (`sequencer.py:1810-1827`).
- The sanctioned cross-thread entries are all marshalling calls onto `_event_loop`: `trigger()` (`composition.py:5224`) delegates to `_schedule_one_shot`, which probes for a running loop and otherwise uses `run_coroutine_threadsafe` (`composition.py:5383-5400`); the live reloader's watcher thread does the same (`subsequence/live_reloader.py:246-255`); MIDI input callbacks use `call_soon_threadsafe` for clock and transport (`sequencer.py:764-788`) but write CC mappings into `composition.data` directly from the rtmidi thread, on the stated ground that "single dict writes are safe from a non-asyncio thread under CPython's GIL" (`sequencer.py:770-772`). `composition.data` is a plain dict (`composition.py:1454`) that every builder receives as `data = composition_ref.data` (`composition.py:6055`); `tweak()` updates a pattern's `_tweaks` dict with no loop check (`composition.py:4129-4151`), `mute()` sets `_muted` likewise (`composition.py:3917-3938`). All three are read at the next rebuild on the loop.
- The existing OSC receiver runs on the clock loop (`AsyncIOOSCUDPServer` with `asyncio.get_event_loop()`, `subsequence/osc.py:82-88`) and its handlers write `composition.data`, call `set_bpm` and `mute` inline (`osc.py:133-194`). python-osc's asyncio server calls `dispatcher.call_handlers_for_packet` from `datagram_received`, so handlers are synchronous and run on the loop (python-osc 1.10.2 as installed in the measurement venv, `pythonosc/osc_server.py:181-184`; the same in the current source, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/attwad/python-osc/master/pythonosc/osc_server.py). Outbound state goes once per bar from a `bar` listener (`composition.py:5489-5500`, registered at `5867`).
- The legacy web UI shows the two ways the repo already keeps work off the clock: its HTTP server is a daemon thread (`subsequence/web_ui.py:117-129`), and its WebSocket broadcast on the loop is throttled to 100 ms and skips unchanged frames because "serialising the full note set 10x/sec on the audio loop is avoidable jitter" (`web_ui.py:175-196`).
- The keystroke listener is the third precedent: a thread collects input and the loop drains it from a `bar` listener (`composition.py:5871-5877`).

What this allows an adapter: a daemon thread for socket I/O, thread-to-loop handoff only through `call_soon_threadsafe` or `run_coroutine_threadsafe` on `_sequencer._event_loop` or through a queue drained by a listener that is already on the loop, and reading pattern state (`live_info()`, `composition.py:3868-3915`; `pattern.steps`) only on the loop. What each of those costs is measured below.

## Subsample: threads, no loop, a written lock order

- Startup is thread orchestration: recorder and player threads are daemons, the main thread waits on `shutdown_event` (`subsample/cli.py:1726-1810`). There is no asyncio loop anywhere in the process.
- The player's threading model is written in its module docstring (`subsample/player.py:7-29`): PortAudio callback thread, rtmidi callback thread, and named locks with an ordering rule (`_rules_lock` outermost, then `_state_lock`; never acquire either while holding `_voices_lock`, `_mix_matrix_lock` or `_cc_debounce_lock`). `run()` opens the input port in callback mode and then only waits for shutdown (`player.py:4262-4280`, `4444-4463`); the virtual port is `mido.open_input(name, virtual=True, callback=...)` (`player.py:4444-4450`). `_safe_handle_message` wraps `_handle_message` because an exception escaping the rtmidi C++ thread would be swallowed silently (`player.py:4872-4889`); `_handle_message` "must stay fast" because a slow handler "stalls MIDI dispatch for the lifetime of the port" (`player.py:4940-4946`).
- Events: `subsample.events.EventEmitter` is lock-guarded, snapshots handlers before calling, and swallows handler exceptions with a warning (`subsample/events.py:20-59`); handlers run "synchronously on the emitting thread" (`events.py:7`). `app_events` is created in `cli.py:1460` and emits `sample_loaded` (`cli.py:2333`) and `sample_captured` (`cli.py:2437`) from recorder and worker threads; `player.events` emits `cc` from the rtmidi thread after taking `_state_lock` (`player.py:5048-5055`).
- Cross-thread reads have a pattern: `_snapshot_cc_state` copies under `_state_lock` and is "required at call sites that run on non-MIDI threads" (`player.py:4728-4748`); `update_assignments` is the public re-evaluation entry, serialised under `_rules_lock`, and documented for calls from the watcher and on-complete threads (`player.py:6135-6165`). The Supervisor adapter read voices under `player._voices_lock` (`supervisor/app/subsample.py:154-166`) and accumulated CC and sample events under its own lock (`supervisor/app/subsample.py:83-90`, `142-150`), reaching into private attributes of another repository.
- The only trigger path is MIDI into the input port; `_trigger_one` takes a `mido.Message` (`player.py:5493-5500`) and the player opens no output port (no `open_output` in `player.py`). A `note_off`, or a `note_on` at velocity zero, releases every matching voice that is not `one_shot` (`player.py:4967-4972`); a `one_shot` voice "plays to natural completion" and ignores note-off (`player.py:3483-3485`, `2684-2685`); CC 123 releases the held non-`one_shot` voices on its channel and CC 120 fades every voice on its channel including `one_shot`s (`player.py:5057-5095`). The OSC receiver is a `ThreadingOSCUDPServer` on a daemon thread accepting only `/sample/import` (`subsample/osc.py:141-176`), loopback by default (`subsample/config.py:543-548`). The Supervisor hook is constructed before the worker threads start and run with `start_threaded()`, its own loop on its own daemon thread (`cli.py:1747-1759`, `supervisor/core.py:91-119`), degrading to a warning on `ImportError` or a busy port (`cli.py:1761-1769`).

What this allows an adapter: a daemon thread of its own (a loop on it if the transport wants one), subscriptions to `app_events` and `player.events` that copy and return, reads through `_snapshot_cc_state` or under the named locks in the documented order, and a control path that is either MIDI into the virtual port or a new public method on `MidiPlayer`. Calling `_safe_handle_message` from a second thread is not covered by the lock model, which was written for one MIDI thread; it needs review before an adapter relies on it. A sender that disappears between a pad's note-on and its note-off leaves a `gated` or `loop` voice sounding until a later note-off or CC 123 on that channel, and leaves a `one_shot` voice unaffected; whatever sends pad MIDI must therefore own the release as well as the press.

## Substation: asyncio main loop, DSP on executor threads, events marshalled by the emitter

- `RadioScanner.scan()` records the running loop (`substation/scanner.py:1947-1948`), streams IQ in an executor (`scanner.py:1953-1960`) and runs `_process_samples` per slice in the default executor with the loop passed in (`scanner.py:1999-2006`), yielding to other tasks between slices "so they don't starve" (`scanner.py:2008-2010`). The device callback marshals samples onto the loop with `call_soon_threadsafe` (`scanner.py:971-1006`). `self.loop` is `None` until `scan()` runs (`scanner.py:299`).
- `RadioScanner.emit()` (`scanner.py:402-439`) has four rules: async handler plus `loop=` is `run_coroutine_threadsafe`; sync handler plus `loop=` is `call_soon_threadsafe`; sync handler without `loop=` is called on the caller's thread; an async handler without `loop=` is silently skipped. Handler exceptions are logged at DEBUG.
- The six events reach a handler by three routes. `recording_started` (`scanner.py:1438`) and `channel_state` (`scanner.py:1892`) are emitted with `loop=` from the executor thread, so a sync handler runs on the loop and an async handler is scheduled on it. `recording_discarded` (`scanner.py:1499`, `1515`) and `recording_saved` (`scanner.py:1521`) are emitted without `loop=` from inside `async def _stop_channel_recording` (`scanner.py:1458`), a coroutine the executor thread hands to the loop with `asyncio.run_coroutine_threadsafe(..., loop)` (`scanner.py:1874-1876`); the comment at `scanner.py:1492-1493` says so ("runs as a coroutine on the event loop, so emit() calls here don't need loop="). A sync handler on these therefore also runs on the loop thread, by the third rule rather than the second, but an async handler registered on `recording_saved` or `recording_discarded` is silently skipped by the fourth rule, so an adapter must register sync handlers for them. `noise_floor` and `channel_snr` are emitted every slice without `loop=` from the executor thread (`scanner.py:1617`, `1899`), so their handlers run on that thread. `channel_state` is deliberately late by one demodulated window so the tone rides on the activation (#1478; `scanner.py:1879-1896`).
- There is no inbound path of any kind: no listening socket in the package, no retune or command method on `RadioScanner`, band fixed at construction (`substation/cli.py:150-155`). The OSC sender is not imported by the CLI and not configured by default (`substation/osc_sender.py:16-20`; no `osc` section in `substation/config.yaml.default`). The Supervisor hook is created with `_start_supervisor` and awaited on the scanner's own loop before `scan()` (`cli.py:157-167`, `240-252`); its `_on_channel_snr` "runs on the scanner's executor thread" (`supervisor/app/substation.py:49-51`) and publishes by atomic reference swap (`supervisor/app/substation.py:111-129`).
- Deploys on Raspberry Pi as well as x86 (`substation/pyproject.toml`, mypy pinned for aarch64).

What this allows an adapter: a client or server on the scanner's loop (the loop is not a clock, so wake-ups are harmless) or on its own thread; sync subscriptions to the four recording and state events, whose handlers land on the loop by one route or the other, and subscriptions to the two per-slice events, whose handlers run on the executor thread and must copy and swap rather than take locks the DSP might wait on; any control path (band switch) is new code in Substation, since nothing exists to call.

Reading:

- The cost is per wake-up of the clock loop, not per byte and not per transport. OSC on the loop, OSC on a thread with `call_soon_threadsafe` per datagram, a WebSocket link on the loop or on a thread, and a TCP link on a thread all land within measurement noise of each other at 50 Hz: p99 between 1.05 and 1.22 ms, maximum between 1.17 and 2.74 ms, 30 to 59 pulses over 1 ms in 1536 across the two blocks. At 5 Hz, a realistic tap rate, the same shapes leave 2 to 4 late pulses in 1536 (p99 0.36 to 0.49 ms) with the same 1.1 ms maximum: fewer wake-ups, the same cost per wake-up.
- Queueing on the thread and applying at the sequencer's own hooks costs nothing measurable, for the OSC server (p99 0.008 ms) and for the WebSocket link the companion design recommends (p99 0.002 ms drained at `beat`, 0.002 ms drained at `reschedule_pulse` and `beat`, 0.001 ms drained only at `reschedule_pulse`), all within a few microseconds of the block's baselines (0.002 and 0.003 ms). The `reschedule_pulse` drain runs inside `_advance_pulse` before that pulse's `_process_pulse`, so its cost falls ahead of that pulse's MIDI rather than in the sleep the jitter log measures; at a hundred dict writes per drain (the `reschedule_pulse`-only row) it did not move the maximum (0.019 ms). The row that drained at both hooks had one pulse at 1.108 ms; the load average had risen from 0.36 to 1.55 during that run from other work on the workstation, and the heavier drain beside it shows no such pulse, so the outlier is read as load, not as the drain.
- The mechanism is the sleep, not the work: CPython's `EpollSelector.select` rounds its timeout up, "epoll_wait() has a resolution of 1 millisecond, round away from zero to wait *at least* timeout seconds" (`math.ceil(timeout * 1e3) * 1e-3`, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/python/cpython/3.13/Lib/selectors.py), while `SelectSelector` passes the timeout through unrounded. When a thread-safe callback or a datagram wakes the loop during the clock's `asyncio.sleep(sleep_time - 0.001)`, the loop re-enters the selector with a fresh, rounded timeout and the sleep resumes past the 1 ms spin margin. Running the identical traffic under a `SelectSelector` loop removes the tail (rows marked select: p99 0.001 to 0.018 ms for every shape at 50 Hz, against 1.05 to 1.16 ms under epoll). This is an engine-side lever (choose the selector, or widen the spin margin) that `subsequence-grid` and `serving` may want; it is not a transport property.
- `beat` runs as a spawned task after the pulse's MIDI, so a drain there applies taps up to one beat after arrival; `reschedule_pulse` is awaited immediately before the rebuild that consumes the grid, so a drain there adds no musical latency over per-message application (#1915 puts the tap-to-sound window at one lookahead to one cycle plus a lookahead regardless).
